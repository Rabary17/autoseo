Tired of driving alone, paying full price for fuel or putting up with public transport delays? Carpooling is the answer. With up to **€900 in annual incentives** if you combine it with a public transport pass, apps for every journey and options to suit every budget, it’s a solution that makes sense. But with state incentives, BlaBlaCar and specialist apps all vying for your attention, how do you choose? Here’s what you need to know to make the right decision.

## Why has carpooling become second nature?

![Two smiling people carpooling in a car in the city](/images/articles/covoiturage-1.webp)

Picture this: you head to work stress-free, without hunting for a parking space, and split the cost of fuel. That’s carpooling. With fuel prices rising and traffic jams getting worse, more and more people in France are embracing it. The government is backing the trend with incentives: up to **€600 a year** in the private sector, **€900** if you combine it with a public transport pass. And let’s not forget the environmental impact—fewer cars on the road means less CO₂.

Carpooling is also about convenience. No more stressing over parking, and you can chat with your driver or fellow passengers during the journey. A daily commute by carpool is often quicker than public transport, especially if you live on the outskirts. And with the incentives on offer, it’s even more cost-effective.

## Carpooling incentives: how to make the most of them

The government and local authorities have introduced several schemes to encourage carpooling. Here’s what you’re entitled to:

-   **€100 carpooling incentive**: for journeys under 80 km, simply sign up to an approved app (like BlaBlaCar Daily or Karos) and complete 10 trips in 3 months.
-   **Sustainable mobility allowance (forfait mobilités durables)**: your employer can pay you up to **€600 a year** (€900 if you combine it with a public transport pass). This allowance is tax- and social-security-exempt.
-   **Local incentives**: some regions or metropolitan areas add their own boost. For example, the Lyon Metropolitan Area offers up to €150 extra for home-to-work journeys.

To take advantage, use an approved app and provide proof of your journeys. Most apps handle everything, including sending the details to the relevant organisations. It’s as easy as that.

## Which app should you choose for your journey?

![Carpooling app interface on a smartphone showing available journeys](/images/articles/covoiturage-2.webp)

Not all carpooling apps are created equal. Some are optimised for short trips, others for long-distance travel. Here’s how to choose:

### For short trips (under 80 km): BlaBlaCar Daily, Karos, Klaxit

If you regularly make the same journey, like your daily commute, apps like BlaBlaCar Daily or Karos are ideal. They’re designed for recurring trips and make it easy to find carpool partners quickly. You can even book in advance and choose regular drivers or passengers for extra peace of mind.

These apps also work with carpooling incentives. For example, with BlaBlaCar Daily, you can combine the €100 incentive with your employer’s sustainable mobility allowance. A real boost for your wallet.

### For long trips (over 80 km): classic BlaBlaCar

If you’re heading off for a weekend or holiday, classic BlaBlaCar is still the go-to. The app offers journeys across France and Europe, and lets you choose your driver based on reviews and preferences (music, conversation, etc.).

Prices are often cheaper than a train ticket, and you can travel with luggage without any hassle. However, state incentives don’t apply to long trips, so it’s less appealing financially.

## Commuting by carpool: how to get organised

![Group of morning carpoolers getting into a car for work](/images/articles/covoiturage-3.webp)

Carpooling for your daily commute is practical, but it does require a bit of organisation. Here are our tips to make it work smoothly:

-   **Find regular carpool partners**: if you make the same journey every day, try to find colleagues or neighbours who live nearby. That way, you won’t have to look for a new driver or passenger every time.
-   **Have a plan B**: if your usual driver is off sick or running late, always have a backup (public transport, bike, etc.).
-   **Stick to the schedule**: carpooling is built on trust. If you’re often late, your carpool partners might not want to travel with you again.
-   **Share the costs**: even if it’s friendly, don’t forget to contribute to fuel and _péage_ (toll) costs. Most apps automatically calculate each person’s share.

If you combine carpooling with public transport, you can benefit from your employer’s sustainable mobility allowance. For example, if you take the metro for part of the journey and carpool for the rest, you could receive up to €900 a year. A great way to cut your transport costs.

## Mistakes to avoid when carpooling

Carpooling is simple, but there are a few pitfalls to watch out for:

-   **Don’t skip the reviews**: before booking a journey, check other users’ feedback. If a driver has negative comments (late, dangerous driving), steer clear.
-   **Don’t forget to confirm the trip**: some apps automatically cancel your booking if you don’t confirm your attendance 24 hours before departure. The result? You’re left without a ride on the day.
-   **Don’t leave it too tight**: if you’ve got an important appointment, always allow a bit of extra time in case the journey takes longer than expected (traffic jams, detours, etc.).
-   **Don’t overlook safety**: even though apps verify profiles, stay cautious. Avoid sharing too much personal information and always let someone know your route.

Finally, follow the rules of common courtesy: be on time, avoid blasting music or bringing strong-smelling food. A pleasant journey is one where everyone feels comfortable.
