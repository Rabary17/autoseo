A road trip in a camper van along the Loire, with two bikes on the roof and a tight budget—where can you stop for the night without the hassle? In France, the options for sleeping in a motorhome or camper van are plentiful, but each comes with its own rules, advantages, and pitfalls. Free stopover areas by the lake, apps that list the most discreet spots, or even nights with locals via France Passion—the choice depends on your mood. Here’s how to avoid the headaches and make the most of the freedom of travel.

## Motorhome Stopover Areas: Free, Paid, or via an App?

![Free motorhome stopover area by a lake, with a camper van parked under the trees](/images/articles/voyager-aires-1.webp)

Motorhome stopover areas are spaces designed for overnight parking. They come in three formats:

-   **Free**: Often on the outskirts of towns or near tourist sites, they offer basic parking, sometimes with a water point or waste disposal station. Example: the stopover area in Saint-Jean-de-Luz, by the sea, where you can spend the night for €0 with an ocean view.
-   **Paid**: For €5 to €15 a night, they provide services like electricity, drinking water, or toilets. Some are managed by networks like Camping-Car Park, with secure access and waste disposal stations.
-   **Via an app**: Platforms like Park4Night or CamperContact list thousands of spots, free or paid, with traveller reviews and photos. Handy for avoiding nasty surprises, especially in peak season.

In France, a motorhome is allowed to park on public roads just like a car, but watch out for the details: it’s illegal to “camp”—that is, to put out chocks, extend an awning, or set up a table and chairs on the pavement. By-laws specifically banning motorhomes are, in fact, deemed illegal by case law, unless the local authority can prove a safety or traffic-related reason.

## France Passion: Sleeping with Locals, Among Vineyards and Farms

![Motorhome parked in a vineyard in autumn, exchange between a winemaker and travellers](/images/articles/voyager-aires-2.webp)

Spending the night in the middle of a Burgundy vineyard or on an organic farm in Provence, with nothing but the sound of cicadas—this is what France Passion offers. It’s a network of farmers, winemakers, and producers who welcome motorhome travellers for a free night in exchange for a purchase on site—a bottle of wine, some cheese, or seasonal fruit.

The principle is simple: you buy the France Passion guide (around €30), which lists over 2,000 stopovers in France. Each address is geolocated, with details of available services (water, electricity, waste disposal) and products on offer. Ideal for travellers looking to meet locals and discover regional specialities. But remember, France Passion isn’t a campsite: hosts don’t always provide toilets, and some spots are just a dirt car park. Make sure you’re self-sufficient, especially in a camper van without built-in toilets.

## Motorhomes and Skiing: Where to Sleep Near Resorts in Winter?

![Motorhome parked near a ski resort in winter, snow and mountains in the background](/images/articles/voyager-aires-3.webp)

Heading to the slopes in a motorhome is possible, but it takes a bit of planning. Ski resorts aren’t always equipped to welcome camper vans, and sub-zero temperatures mean taking precautions: winterising the vehicle, insulation, heating. Here are the solutions for sleeping near the pistes without any nasty surprises.

-   **Dedicated parking areas**: Some resorts, like Les Menuires or Val Thorens, offer car parks specifically for motorhomes, with waste disposal stations and non-frozen water points. Count on €10 to €25 a night.
-   **Campsites open in winter**: Rare, but some mountain campsites stay open in the cold season. They offer heated toilets and electricity, but prices rise to between €20 and €40 a night.
-   **Wild camping in remote spots**: Tolerated in some areas, but banned in national parks and nature reserves. Check signs and local by-laws before settling in for the night.

Before you set off, prepare your vehicle for winter. Drain the water tanks, insulate the pipes, and pack an auxiliary heater if your motorhome isn’t equipped with a high-performance system. A frozen battery or a waste water tank blocked by ice can quickly ruin a mountain getaway.

## How to Choose Between These Solutions? 3 Key Criteria

Between free stopover areas, apps, France Passion, and campsites, the choice depends on your priorities. Here are three criteria to help you decide:

| Criteria | Free areas | Apps (Park4Night, CamperContact) | France Passion | Campsites |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Budget** | €0 | €0 to €15/night | Free (guide €30) | €10 to €40/night |
| **Services** | Basic (water, waste disposal) | Varies (water, electricity, toilets) | None (unless you buy on site) | Full (showers, electricity, Wi-Fi) |
| **Atmosphere** | Self-sufficient, often remote | Traveller community | Local encounters, regional produce | Family-friendly, touristy |

*Source: Vroomly*

For example, for a weekend in a camper van with two bikes, a free stopover area by a lake is perfect if you’re after peace and quiet. On the other hand, if you’re travelling with young children, a campsite with toilets and a play area will be more suitable. Finally, for an autumn road trip through the vineyards, France Passion is an ideal option for combining discovery and local encounters.

## Mistakes to Avoid When Looking for a Place to Sleep in a Motorhome

Even with the best apps and the most comprehensive guides, a few pitfalls can ruin a night in a motorhome. Here are five to avoid:

-   **Relying solely on online reviews**: A spot rated five stars in summer can turn into a quagmire in winter or be overrun by mosquitoes in June. Check recent reviews and photos for a realistic idea.
-   **Forgetting to check local restrictions**: Some towns ban overnight motorhome parking, even in designated areas. A quick look at signs or a search on Park4Night can save you a fine.
-   **Overlooking your vehicle’s self-sufficiency**: A free stopover area in the middle of the countryside won’t have water or electricity. Make sure you have enough supplies (water, gas, battery) to last 24 to 48 hours without services.
-   **Parking without respecting basic courtesy**: Avoid parking in front of someone’s house, making noise after 10 pm, or leaving rubbish behind. Conflicts with locals are one of the main reasons for restrictions on motorhomes.
-   **Ignoring the weather**: A stopover area by a river can flood after heavy rain, and a mountain car park can become impassable in snow. Check the forecast before choosing your spot.

Before you set off, also check that your insurance covers theft or damage to bikes fixed to a bike rack. Most standard car insurance policies don’t cover accessories like bike racks, let alone the bikes themselves. An extension to your policy may be necessary for peace of mind.

## Finally, Which Solution Should You Choose for Your Next Road Trip?

The choice of where to sleep in a motorhome or camper van depends above all on your priorities:

-   **For a tight budget**: Free stopover areas and apps like Park4Night are your best bet. Opt for spots with recent reviews to avoid nasty surprises.
-   **To meet locals and discover regional produce**: France Passion is a unique option, perfect for travellers who enjoy encounters and local products.
-   **For a family trip or with young children**: Campsites offer suitable services (toilets, play areas, activities), but at a higher price.
-   **For a winter road trip**: Choose secure stopover areas near ski resorts or campsites open all year. Remember to winterise your vehicle before you go.

Whatever option you choose, one golden rule: respect the places and other users. A motorhome traveller who leaves their spot clean and follows parking rules helps maintain the community’s reputation and prevents further restrictions.
