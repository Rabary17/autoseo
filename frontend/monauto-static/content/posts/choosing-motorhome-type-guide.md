***A 400 km weekend away as a couple doesn't call for the same vehicle as a three-week family tour of Europe. The choice between low-profile, coachbuilt and over-cab motorhomes comes down to three factors: unladen weight, interior space and fuel consumption.***

A low-profile weighs around 2.8 tonnes unladen, against 3.5 tonnes for a comparable coachbuilt. That 700 kg gap shows up at the pump, at the toll booth, and sometimes even in which licence you need.

TL;DR: the right choice depends mostly on group size and distance travelled.

-   The **low-profile** stays the most economical to run, ideal for a couple or short trips.
-   The **coachbuilt** offers the most space and comfort, at the cost of higher fuel consumption and purchase price.
-   The **over-cab** remains the most accessible family option, thanks to its raised bed above the cab.

## What's the difference between low-profile, coachbuilt and over-cab?

**Direct answer**: the low-profile has a flat roof with no raised cab, the coachbuilt extends the cab into the living area for maximum space, and the over-cab adds a bed above the driving position. Each shape strikes a different balance between weight, space and budget.

### Low-profile: the weight-space balance for short trips

The low-profile is recognisable by its flat roof and lack of a raised cab, which gives it a **low centre of gravity** and reduced wind resistance. The result is the most controlled fuel consumption of the three shapes, a clear advantage on a trip like Lyon to Marseille.

![Low-profile motorhome parked on a coastal road with two bikes on the roof](/images/articles/profile-vs-integral-vs-capucine-1.webp)

Interior space stays generous for its category, with enough headroom for a couple and one child, though tighter for four adults. Recent models include modular beds and compact kitchens, designed more for weekends than long stays.

**Strengths**: lightest unladen weight of the three shapes, reduced fuel consumption, more accessible purchase budget.
**Weaknesses**: limited sleeping space for a large family, average thermal insulation, narrow cab for taller drivers.

### Coachbuilt: premium comfort for long stays

The coachbuilt motorhome prioritises space and comfort. Its forward cab and high roof offer the most generous living volume of the three shapes, often with a **separate shower**, a fixed central bed and a lounge that converts into a spare bedroom.

![Interior of a coachbuilt motorhome with a family at the table, view of a mountainous landscape](/images/articles/profile-vs-integral-vs-capucine-2.webp)

On a road trip of several thousand kilometres, the difference is clear: less fatigue, more autonomy for water and electricity. In exchange, the higher purchase price is justified by premium features, dual gas-and-electric heating, solar panels, a larger fridge, though fuel consumption rises noticeably compared with a low-profile.

**Strengths**: maximum living space, premium features, extended autonomy for long stays.
**Weaknesses**: heaviest unladen weight, highest fuel consumption, reduced manoeuvrability in town and on narrow roads.

### Over-cab: the budget-friendly family option

The over-cab stands out with its **raised bed** above the cab, an economical way to sleep children or friends without sacrificing lounge space. It generally costs less than an equivalent coachbuilt, while offering a comparable living volume.

The weight stays reasonable, but consumption and wind resistance penalise long trips slightly compared with a low-profile. Access to the over-cab bed, often via a ladder, stays less practical day to day, and its taller overall height sometimes complicates parking in town.

**Strengths**: extra bed without encroaching on lounge space, accessible purchase budget, a good compromise for a family with children.
**Weaknesses**: higher consumption than a low-profile, less practical bed access, often mediocre thermal insulation.

## Which model suits your actual use?

For a couple who travels occasionally, a **low-profile** is more than enough: its fixed bed and compact kitchen avoid daily set-up, and its annual fuel budget stays the most contained of the three shapes.

A family with two children will more naturally lean towards an **over-cab**: the raised bed frees up lounge space for meals, for a purchase budget that stays more accessible than an equivalent coachbuilt.

For a multi-week tour of Europe, the **coachbuilt** is usually the best choice: its insulated double floor and water capacity guarantee the autonomy needed over the long haul, at the cost of a higher fuel budget.

## Maintenance and reliability: what really costs over time

Motorhomes share the same weak points as commercial vans: suspension, braking and tyres. An annual service covering gas, electricity and the water system is recommended across all three shapes, and even more so on a coachbuilt, whose mechanical complexity naturally raises the five-year maintenance bill.

The most common faults involve heating, onboard electronics and the exhaust system. On insurance, the three shapes don't carry the same risk: a wider, taller coachbuilt generally costs more to insure than a compact low-profile. ## Frequently asked questions

### What's the main difference between a low-profile and a coachbuilt?

The low-profile has a flat roof with no raised cab, a low centre of gravity and reduced fuel consumption. The coachbuilt extends the cab into the living area for maximum space, at the cost of higher weight and consumption.

### Which shape uses the least fuel?

The low-profile, thanks to its reduced wind resistance and low centre of gravity. The coachbuilt remains the thirstiest shape, followed by the over-cab.

### Does an over-cab suit a family with two children?

Yes, it's often the best compromise: the raised bed houses the children without encroaching on lounge space, for a purchase budget more accessible than an equivalent coachbuilt.

### Which motorhome should you choose for a multi-week tour of Europe?

The coachbuilt remains the best suited to long stays thanks to its water and electricity autonomy, despite a higher fuel budget than the other two shapes.

### Does unladen weight affect which licence you need to drive a motorhome?

Yes, the authorised loaded weight determines the licence required. Always check this figure before buying, particularly for a coachbuilt, generally heavier than a low-profile or an over-cab.

### Does a coachbuilt motorhome cost more to insure than a low-profile?

Generally yes, its larger size and higher value lead to a higher premium than a compact low-profile of the same age.
