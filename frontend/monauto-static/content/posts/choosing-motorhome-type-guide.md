A 400 km weekend with two bikes on the roof? A three-week family tour of Europe? The choice between low-profile, coachbuilt or over-cab motorhomes comes down to three factors: unladen weight, interior space and fuel consumption. A low-profile weighs 2.8 tonnes, compared to 3.5 for a coachbuilt. Those 700 kg make a difference at the pump and at the toll booths.

## 1\. Low-profile: the weight-space balance for short trips

The low-profile is recognisable by its flat roof and lack of a raised cab. The result: a low centre of gravity and reduced wind resistance. This keeps fuel consumption under control.

On a Lyon-Marseille trip (320 km), a low-profile consumes 12.5 L/100 km, compared to 15.2 for a similarly sized coachbuilt. That’s a saving of £8.64 per trip (SP95 at €1.80/L).

![Low-profile motorhome parked on a coastal road with two bikes on the roof](/images/articles/profile-vs-integral-vs-capucine-1.webp)

The interior space remains generous: 6 to 8 m² of living area for 2.5 to 3 m in length, with a ceiling height of 1.90 m. Enough for a couple with one child, but tight for four adults. Recent models like the [Pilote P620](/en/motorhomes-campervans/buying-guide) or the Rapido 640 feature modular beds and compact kitchens, ideal for weekends away.

### Strengths

-   Unladen weight: 2.5 to 3 tonnes (MAM 3.5 t)
-   Fuel consumption: 11 to 13 L/100 km
-   New price: €55,000 to €75,000
-   Ideal for: couples, trips under 1,000 km, winding roads

### Weaknesses

-   Limited sleeping space for families
-   Average thermal insulation (cold in winter, hot in summer)
-   Narrow cab for taller drivers (> 1.85 m)

## 2\. Coachbuilt: premium comfort for long stays

The coachbuilt motorhome prioritises space and comfort. Its forward cab and high roof offer 8 to 10 m² of living area, with a ceiling height of 2.10 m. Enough room for a separate shower, a fixed central bed and a lounge that converts into a guest bedroom.

On a 3,000 km road trip, the difference is noticeable. Less fatigue, more autonomy. However, the higher purchase price (€80,000 to €120,000) is justified by premium features: gas and electric heating, 300 W solar panels, 150 L refrigerator.

Fuel consumption, however, increases: 14 to 17 L/100 km. On a Paris-Nice trip (1,080 km), expect to spend €151 more on fuel than with a low-profile.

![Interior of a coachbuilt motorhome with a family at the table, view of a mountainous landscape](/images/articles/profile-vs-integral-vs-capucine-2.webp)

### Strengths

-   Living space: 8 to 10 m²
-   Ceiling height: 2.10 m
-   Features: dual heating, instant hot water, off-grid electricity
-   Ideal for: families, long stays, group travel

### Weaknesses

-   Unladen weight: 3.2 to 3.8 tonnes (MAM 4.5 t)
-   Fuel consumption: 14 to 17 L/100 km
-   New price: €80,000 to €120,000
-   Reduced manoeuvrability in towns and on narrow roads

## 3\. Over-cab: the budget-friendly family option

The over-cab motorhome stands out with its raised bed above the cab. An economical solution for accommodating two children or friends without sacrificing lounge space. At an average of €65,000, it costs €15,000 less than an equivalent coachbuilt, while offering 7 to 9 m² of living space.

The weight remains reasonable (2.8 to 3.3 tonnes), but fuel consumption (13 to 15 L/100 km) and wind resistance penalise long trips. On a Bordeaux-Toulouse journey (250 km), a model like the [Challenger Genesis 650](/en/motorhomes-campervans/buying-guide) consumes 3.25 L more than a low-profile. That’s a €5.85 difference per trip.

### Strengths

-   Extra bed without encroaching on lounge space
-   Price: €50,000 to €75,000
-   Living space: 7 to 9 m²
-   Ideal for: families with children, tight budgets

### Weaknesses

-   Fuel consumption: 13 to 15 L/100 km
-   Awkward access to the over-cab bed (ladder)
-   Poor thermal insulation (cold in winter)
-   Total height (3.20 m), causing parking issues

## 5\. Which model for which use?

A couple covering 5,000 km a year will find a low-profile like the **Adria Matrix 670 SL** (2.9 t, 12 L/100 km) sufficient. Its fixed central bed and compact kitchen eliminate daily set-up. Annual fuel budget: €1,080.

A family with two children might opt for an over-cab like the **Chausson Flash 07** (3.1 t, 14 L/100 km). The over-cab bed frees up lounge space for meals, and the price (€62,000) remains affordable. Annual fuel budget: €1,260.

For a three-month European tour, a coachbuilt like the **Hymer B-MC 680** (3.6 t, 16 L/100 km) is the best choice. Its double-insulated floor and 200 L water tank ensure autonomy. Fuel budget for 10,000 km: €2,880.

## 6\. Maintenance and reliability: what really costs

Motorhomes share the same weak points as commercial vehicles: suspension, braking and tyres. A set of 225/75 R16 tyres costs €800 (fitting included), and an oil change plus filters comes to €250. Recent models limit breakdowns, but electric seat rails and air conditioning remain sensitive points.

For motorhome-specific maintenance, an annual service (gas, electricity, water) costs €300. Here are the most common breakdowns:

-   Heating: €400 repair (thermostat or burner).
-   Electronics: €200 (VSS speed sensor, codes P0500/P0503).
-   Exhaust: €100 to €400 (silencer or full system).

Over five years, budget €3,000 to €5,000 for maintenance. A coachbuilt costs 20% more to maintain than a low-profile due to its mechanical complexity.
