At the dealership, « automatic gearbox » is often treated as a single block. In the workshop, we see three distinct mechanisms that do not age the same way. Knowing which one sits in your car avoids surprises at the first service.

TL;DR: the classic automatic is the smoothest and most forgiving. The dual-clutch reacts quickly but needs regular maintenance. The CVT is quiet and economical, with a driving feel that surprises many people.

-   Classic automatic: smooth shifts, simple and well-known maintenance.
-   Dual-clutch: very fast shifts, sensitive to maintenance.
-   CVT: continuous variation, no fixed ratios, a particular driving feel.

## Three families, three logics

The classic automatic relies on a torque converter and a set of planetary gears. It shifts smoothly and copes well with hill starts. It is the oldest and most common design across several generations of models.

The dual-clutch, known as DSG in its Volkswagen group version, uses two clutches operated alternately. The next gear is already prepared, which makes shifts very fast. Some versions, however, can judder at low speed in traffic.

The CVT, or continuously variable transmission, has no fixed gear ratios. It adjusts the ratio constantly through pulleys and a belt. The engine often stays in its most economical range, but the driving feel differs from a classic gearbox.

## What changes day to day

In town, the classic automatic fades into the background. The dual-clutch responds more sharply to the accelerator, which suits drivers who enjoy responsiveness. The CVT is discreet and smooth, although it can feel like slipping during hard acceleration.

On the road, the gap widens. The dual-clutch handles overtaking well. The CVT favours fuel economy. The classic automatic remains the most balanced choice for varied journeys, especially when towing.

## Maintenance and reliability, the point that matters

The dual-clutch requires gearbox oil changes carried out properly, with the oil specified by the manufacturer. A neglected unit can wear out early. The CVT also needs a specific fluid, and it is best not to mix products.

The classic automatic is the most tolerant of less rigorous maintenance, although it remains sensitive to old oil. In every case, ask for the oil change history before buying a used car.

## Which choice for your use

If you drive mostly in town and want a car without surprises, the classic automatic suits you best. If you enjoy sharp acceleration and accept strict maintenance, the dual-clutch may please you. If your priority is fuel economy on regular trips, the CVT is a good candidate, provided you like its particular driving feel.

### Is the DSG a fragile gearbox?

Not in itself. A dual-clutch is reliable when maintained properly. Problems mostly come from a missed oil change or very demanding driving at low speed.

### Is a CVT less reliable than a classic automatic?

Not necessarily, but it needs a specific fluid and consistent maintenance. Check the car’s history before deciding.

### Do you need an automatic gearbox for city driving?

Many drivers find an automatic more comfortable in traffic. The choice depends mainly on your maintenance budget and how much you enjoy driving.
