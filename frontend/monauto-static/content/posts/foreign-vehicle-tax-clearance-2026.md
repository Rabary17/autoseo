To register a vehicle purchased abroad in France, the **tax clearance certificate (quitus fiscal)** is mandatory. This free document, issued by the French tax authorities, confirms that your vehicle complies with French tax regulations. Without it, you cannot obtain a French registration certificate (carte grise).

## 1\. What is the tax clearance certificate?

The tax clearance certificate (quitus fiscal) proves that your foreign vehicle meets French tax obligations. It remains valid indefinitely, unless the vehicle is resold. Only vehicles subject to VAT or specific taxes (cars, motorcycles, commercial vehicles) require this certificate.

## 2\. Which vehicles require a tax clearance certificate?

The tax clearance certificate is required for:

-   New or used vehicles purchased in the **European Union**.
-   Vehicles imported from a **non-EU country**, such as Morocco, Switzerland, or the UK. Customs clearance is required in these cases.
-   Classic vehicles, unless specifically exempt.

Exemptions include:

-   Trailers and semi-trailers (≤ 500 kg).
-   Agricultural vehicles, such as tractors or machinery.
-   Diplomatic or consular vehicles.
-   Vehicles previously registered in France before export.

For vehicles from outside the EU, a **customs clearance certificate (846 A)** is also required. This document, issued by French customs after payment of duties, complements the tax clearance certificate.

## 3\. Where and how to apply for a tax clearance certificate in 2026

Contact the **Business Tax Service (Service des Impôts des Entreprises – SIE)** for your area. Here are the steps:

1.  **Prepare the documents**:
    
    -   Form **n°13750\*08** (Cerfa).
    -   A copy of the **foreign registration certificate**.
    -   Purchase invoice or sales contract.
    
    -   Proof of address **less than 6 months old**.
    -   For non-EU vehicles: **customs certificate 846 A**.
    -   ID (national identity card or passport).
2.  **Submit your application**:
    -   Online via the French tax website or in person at the SIE.
    -   Processing time: **2 to 5 working days** for a complete application.
3.  **Receive your tax clearance certificate**:
    -   The document will be sent by email or post.
    -   Present it to the [ANTS](/en/vehicle-registration-legal) (French National Agency for Secure Documents) to finalise registration.

## 4\. Special cases and mistakes to avoid

For a vehicle imported from a **non-EU country**, ensure the customs clearance certificate (846 A) is included in your application. Without it, the tax clearance certificate will be refused.

Vehicles previously registered in France before export are exempt from the tax clearance certificate. In this case, proof of prior registration is sufficient. For trailers ≤ 500 kg, no application is required — the [European Certificate of Conformity (COC)](/en/vehicle-registration-legal/european-coc-vehicle-registration) is enough.

## 5\. What to do if your tax clearance certificate is refused

A refusal may result from:

-   An incomplete application.
-   Unpaid taxes on the vehicle.
-   An error on the invoice or foreign registration certificate.

The tax authorities will explain the reasons for refusal and the steps to rectify the situation. If in doubt, consult [our guide to registering imported vehicles](/en/vehicle-registration-legal/register-imported-vehicle-france).

## 6\. EU vs non-EU imports: the tax clearance certificate is just one step

The tax clearance certificate is part of a broader process, which varies depending on the vehicle’s origin. For a detailed breakdown of the steps, see our [Import & Registration guide](/en/vehicle-registration-legal/import-registration).

-   **Vehicle from the European Union**: VAT is usually already paid in the country of origin (or in France at purchase if the seller is a private individual and the vehicle is over 6 months old/has travelled over 6,000 km). The tax clearance certificate is still required to verify your status, but no customs duties or clearance apply.
-   **Vehicle from a non-EU country** (UK, Switzerland, Morocco, etc.): Prior customs clearance is necessary, including import duties and VAT calculated on the vehicle’s value, plus any CO₂-related taxes. The tax clearance certificate is only issued after customs clearance — never before.

In both cases, expect a significantly longer process than a standard registration. For non-EU vehicles, in particular, the application cannot progress until customs have completed their part.

## 7\. While waiting for the final registration: the temporary WW plate

While your full application (tax clearance certificate, certificate of conformity or individual vehicle approval, French MOT) is being processed, an imported vehicle can be driven with a temporary registration plate in the WW format. This is issued by an authorised professional for a limited period. It does not replace the final registration process — it simply allows you to use the vehicle while the application is being processed.

The cost of this temporary plate varies depending on the professional issuing it. Check with them beforehand, as this fee is in addition to the overall import budget (tax clearance certificate is free, but regional tax, potential environmental penalty, and — for non-EU vehicles — import duties and VAT may apply).

## 8\. Insuring the vehicle before receiving the French registration certificate

Contrary to popular belief, you don’t need to wait for the final registration certificate to insure an imported vehicle. Most insurers will issue a policy based on the foreign registration certificate and purchase invoice, updating the details once the French certificate is received. Never drive — even with a temporary plate — without valid insurance. Penalties (fines, licence suspension, vehicle impoundment) apply regardless of whether you have a registration certificate.

Always inform your insurer that the vehicle is imported when taking out the policy. They may request accident history or records from the country of origin, and failing to disclose this information could complicate any future claims.
