Open the file of an electric car and the spec sheet talks about range and power. It rarely says which chemistry sits under the floor. Yet that chemistry decides how the battery ages and what you should do to protect it.

TL;DR: LFP costs less and copes better with full charges. NMC stores more energy for the same weight, but needs more thermal care.

-   LFP: more robust, less energy-dense, often cheaper.
-   NMC: more range for the same weight, more sensitive to heat.
-   In both cases, the pack’s thermal management matters as much as the chemistry.

## Two chemistries, two philosophies

LFP uses iron and phosphate. It handles repeated charging cycles well and stays stable when overheated. Its main drawback is lower energy density: for the same weight, it stores less energy than an NMC.

NMC combines nickel, manganese and cobalt. It packs more energy into the same volume, which explains the strong range of some models. The trade-off is a higher raw-material cost and less tolerance to strong heat without careful cooling.

## Charging, where the difference is felt

Day to day, LFP is more forgiving. Several manufacturers suggest charging it to 100% now and then, for example once a week, so the range estimate stays accurate. You can charge it fully without worrying about premature wear.

With an NMC, the cautious habit is to stay around 20 to 80% for everyday trips and keep full charges for long journeys. It is not an absolute rule, but it extends the battery’s useful life.

## Price, range and trade-offs

LFP is usually cheaper to make, which sometimes shows in the price of entry-level versions. NMC costs more and makes sense when range matters more than budget, for example if you drive a lot on motorways.

Do not compare range figures alone. Look at the price of the exact version you want and the way you will really use it. A smaller battery used well can suit a commute of a few dozen kilometres.

## Lifespan and warranty

Both chemistries age, but not at the same pace or in the same way. What matters to you is the battery’s state of health, meaning its remaining capacity, and the manufacturer warranty that covers it. Ask for the exact figure and the warranty duration before signing.

A workshop tip: avoid leaving the car in extreme heat or fully empty for long periods. Both extremes wear a battery faster than the chemistry itself.

### Can you choose between LFP and NMC when buying?

Rarely. The chemistry depends on the model and version. Some manufacturers offer both depending on trim level, so check the spec sheet of the exact version before comparing prices.

### Should you charge an LFP to 100%?

Manufacturers using LFP often recommend an occasional full charge to recalibrate the display. For daily use, partial charging is perfectly fine.

### Does an NMC battery wear out faster?

Not necessarily. A well-cooled NMC used within a reasonable range ages properly. Usage habits such as heat, repeated fast charging and deep discharges often weigh more than the chemistry.
