***An LPG tank runs under pressure. Its periodic inspection, separate from the standard roadworthiness test, is your best protection against a leak or a refused insurance claim.***

TL;DR: how often you need an inspection depends on the tank's installation date and type, shown on the label fitted to it. Expect to pay between €80 and €150 for a full check by an approved professional.

-   LPG tank inspection is **separate from the standard roadworthiness test**: the two stay complementary, not interchangeable.
-   A **leak test** catches invisible leaks before they turn dangerous.
-   An incident linked to an unchecked tank can mean your insurer **refuses to pay out**.

## Why inspect your LPG tank regularly?

**A poorly maintained LPG tank exposes you to leaks, corrosion and, in the worst cases, explosion.** French regulations require a regular inspection to keep the vehicle safe and protect other road users.

Some insurers refuse to cover a claim when it traces back to a tank whose periodic inspection was overdue. That point is worth checking directly with your own policy rather than brushing it aside. For an LPG vehicle, this inspection stays separate from the standard roadworthiness test, and covers checks specific to this kind of fuel system.

Consider a case that comes up more often than you'd think: a motorhome owner whose insurance claim gets turned down after a fire, because the leak traced back to a tank that had never been checked since it was installed. A dated inspection certificate would have changed the outcome, or at least given the owner grounds to argue the point with the insurer.

## When should you have your LPG tank inspected?

**The frequency depends on your tank's installation date and model, both shown on the label fitted to the tank itself.**

-   Tanks installed before 2018: check the deadline printed on the label.
-   Tanks installed after 2018: the frequency can vary by model.

Note: the standard roadworthiness test does not cover this inspection. The two checks stay separate and each run on their own schedule.

## Where to have your LPG tank inspected?

### Approved centres and the options available

The inspection must be carried out by an approved professional. Several options exist.

-   Approved LPG centres, which specialise in installation and inspection.
-   Car dealerships, some of which offer the service.
-   Independent garages, provided you check their LPG approval before booking.

To find a centre near you, check specialist directories. Some insurers also run partnerships with approved centres. For a commercial vehicle running on LPG, ask the centre whether it also handles the fleet-specific paperwork, since a van used for work often carries extra administrative requirements on top of the tank check itself.

### How much does an LPG tank inspection cost?

The price varies by centre and tank type. Expect to pay between **€80 and €150** for a full inspection, covering:

-   A visual and dimensional check of the tank.
-   A leak test, using helium or nitrogen.
-   A check of the accessories: valves, hoses, connectors.
-   A compliance certificate, issued if everything checks out.

## What happens during the inspection?

The inspection follows several steps, all carried out by an approved technician.

-   **Administrative check**: registration document, tank compliance certificate and service log.
-   **Visual inspection**: the tank's general condition, its mountings and protective covers.
-   **Leak test**: pressurised with a tracer gas to detect leaks.
-   **Accessory check**: valves, hoses, connectors and cap.

If the tank passes, the technician issues a certificate. Keep it somewhere safe.

### What if the tank fails?

If the technician finds faults, you'll receive a non-compliance report and a set deadline to carry out repairs.

-   Minor repairs: replacing a hose or valve, €50 to €200.
-   Major repairs: replacing the tank, €1,000 to €2,500.

A follow-up inspection is required after repairs before you get the certificate.

## What are the risks of skipping the inspection?

Skipping your LPG tank's inspection exposes you to both legal and practical risks.

-   Possible administrative penalties.
-   Your insurer refusing to pay out after a claim.
-   The vehicle being immobilised during a roadside check.
-   A higher risk of accident from an undetected gas leak.

To avoid these issues, stick to the inspection deadlines. Our guide to [LPG cars' pros and cons](/lpg-car-pros-cons/) can help you weigh these added obligations against the fuel savings.

## How to prepare your vehicle for the inspection

To make the inspection go smoothly, follow these steps before visiting the approved centre.

-   Check the tank's general condition: dents, corrosion.
-   Inspect the accessories: cap, hoses, valves.
-   Prepare your documents: registration certificate, compliance certificate.
-   Clean the tank to make the visual inspection easier.

## Why this requirement is stricter than for a standard vehicle

An LPG tank runs under pressure. Unlike a petrol or diesel tank, even a small crack or a weakened weld can turn a slow leak into a serious incident, especially in an enclosed space like an underground car park. That's why a dedicated inspection is required, separate from the standard roadworthiness test and carried out by a professional trained for this kind of equipment.

An LPG tank isn't just an accessory. It's a core part of the vehicle's safety system, along with the hoses and valves around it. A single fault on any of these parts can invalidate the whole inspection, even if the tank itself checks out fine.

### Keeping track of your inspections

Every inspection produces a dated document, to keep alongside the vehicle's service log. In a roadside check or after an incident, this proof shows you've met the requirement. Many owners lose this document over the years; it's worth keeping a digital copy alongside the original.

## What to do before selling an LPG-equipped vehicle

If the tank's inspection is coming due, have it done before the sale rather than after. A prospective buyer will almost always ask for the date of the last inspection and its certificate. An overdue tank gives a buyer grounds to negotiate the price down, or to walk away from the deal entirely.

The tank's compliance certificate and the label showing its installation date must go to the new owner along with the rest of the vehicle's paperwork.

### Common misconceptions about LPG tank inspections

Some drivers think a recently installed tank doesn't need inspecting. That's wrong: the installation date never exempts you from the periodic check the regulation requires. Others assume the standard roadworthiness test already covers it. It doesn't either; the two checks stay separate and each run on their own schedule.

Some drivers also put off the inspection, thinking a small leak isn't serious as long as it stays invisible. That's exactly what the leak test exists to catch, before the leak becomes noticeable, and before it becomes dangerous.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the LPG tank inspection replace the standard roadworthiness test?

No. The two checks stay separate and complementary. The roadworthiness test covers the whole vehicle, while the tank inspection specifically targets the LPG system and its tightness.

### What happens if I never have my LPG tank inspected?

You risk a penalty, a refused insurance payout if a claim traces back to the tank, and a higher risk of accident from an undetected leak.

### How much does an LPG tank inspection cost in 2026?

Expect to pay between €80 and €150 for a full check by an approved professional, including the leak test and the accessory check.

### Does a recently installed LPG tank still need inspecting?

Yes. The installation date never exempts you from the periodic check the regulation requires, whatever the tank's age.

### Who can carry out an LPG tank inspection?

An approved LPG centre, some car dealerships, or an independent garage holding the right approval. Check that approval before booking.
