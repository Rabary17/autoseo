## How are motorhomes classified at tolls?

**Toll operators classify each vehicle by height and maximum authorised weight. A Class 2 vehicle, between 2 and 3 metres tall, pays 1.7 times the Class 1 car rate.** Most campervans and compact motorhomes fall into this category.

The classification follows a simple logic: a taller vehicle takes up more space on the road and wears infrastructure down faster. Rates rise accordingly, and the **difference adds up quickly** on a long summer trip.

| Class | Height | Max weight | Vehicle examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | < 2 m | < 3.5 t | Cars, motorbikes, small vans |
| 2 | 2–3 m | < 3.5 t | Campervans, compact motorhomes |
| 3 | > 3 m | < 3.5 t | Coachbuilt motorhomes, minibuses |
| 4 | > 3 m | 3.5–12 t | HGVs, coaches |
| 5 | All | > 12 t | Lorries, articulated vehicles |

*Source: VINCI Autoroutes classification, toll operators' rate schedules*

Always measure your vehicle's actual height, satellite dish and bike rack included. A motorhome over 3 metres tips into Class 3, and the bill rises by 30 to 50% on the same route.

## How much does a Class 2 motorhome pay at French tolls?

**A Class 2 motorhome pays around 1.7 times the car rate on French motorways. On a Paris-Marseille trip, expect close to €110 against €65 for a car, about €45 more for the same route.**

Three real examples for a summer road trip:

-   **Paris → Marseille (A6/A7)**: around €110 (against €65 for a car).
-   **Lyon → Bordeaux (A89)**: around €95 (against €56 for a car).
-   **Strasbourg → Montpellier (A36/A7)**: around €105 (against €62 for a car).

These figures include annual inflation-linked increases but exclude surcharges for roadworks or special events. To estimate your route precisely, use the toll calculator run by the relevant motorway operator.

### Three tried-and-tested tips to pay less

-   **Avoid peak hours.** Some tolls apply a reduced rate at night or at weekends, sometimes 10% less between 10pm and 6am.
-   **Compare against free roads.** National and secondary roads add time but skip the toll. Check the detail in our article on [motorhome speed limits](/motorhome-speed-limits-law/).
-   **Get an electronic tag subscription.** A tag like Liber-t cuts the bill by 5 to 10% for regular drivers.

## What changes across Europe for a Class 2 motorhome?

Every country runs its own system, and a Class 2 motorhome isn't always treated the way it is in France. Before a multi-week road trip, check the system in force in each country you'll cross: vignette, electronic toll or traditional barrier.

### Hungary: the e-matrica electronic vignette

Hungary has no physical toll barriers. An **electronic vignette** (e-matrica) stays mandatory for any vehicle up to 3.5 tonnes. The annual rate for category D1 (Class 2 motorhomes) sits around €168, with 10-day (around €19) or monthly (around €30) options also available. You buy it online, and it's linked to your number plate. Checks run by camera, with no tolerance for a missed vignette.

### Poland: targeted tolls, no national vignette

Poland doesn't impose a national vignette, but certain motorway sections (A1, A2, A4) still charge at a traditional toll booth. The 40% increase applied in early 2026 only concerns HGVs and coaches over 3.5 tonnes, which fall under the e-TOLL system. A Class 2 motorhome isn't subject to that hike and pays a stable rate, around €5 on the Gdańsk–Toruń section for example.

### Turkey: fully electronic, no cash accepted

Turkish tolls run entirely on two electronic systems, the OGS transponder and the HGS card. Without one of the two, you can't get through. Expect around €0.50 for an Edirne–Istanbul trip and around €1.50 to cross a Bosphorus bridge. A Class 2 motorhome is classed in category 2, the same as a delivery van.

### Romania: the Rovinieta vignette, the cheapest in the region

For vehicles up to 3.5 tonnes (category A), the CNAIR rates updated in 2025 sit around €6 for 10 days, €9.50 for 30 days or €50 for a year. It's the most affordable option among the countries covered here, ideal for a road trip through the Carpathians. The vignette is linked to your plate and checked by camera.

### Greece: a barrier system close to the French model

Greece runs a traditional toll system, paid in cash or by card at each barrier. A Class 2 motorhome pays a modest surcharge over a car, around €30 on the Athens–Thessaloniki route against €20 for a car.

## Mistakes to avoid with a Class 2 motorhome

Travelling by motorhome means real freedom, provided you avoid a few common pitfalls, especially on your first trip with a vehicle this size.

-   **Overlooking the exact height.** A vehicle at 3.01 metres tips into Class 3, with the toll rate rising by 30 to 50%. Measure it with all accessories fitted.
-   **Ignoring mandatory electronic systems.** In Turkey or Hungary, no cash payment is possible. Without a transponder or vignette, you block traffic and risk a fine.
-   **Driving without the right insurance.** A Class 2 motorhome needs cover that includes accessories like a bike rack, often excluded from standard policies.
-   **Parking without checking local rules.** Some municipal bylaws restrict motorhome parking, even where their legality is debatable. Check our article on [parking rules in towns](/motorhome-parking-city-rules/) before you set off.
-   **Underestimating the loaded weight.** The maximum authorised weight also determines which licence you need. Our guide to [motorhome weight and licence rules](/motorhome-weight-licence-guide/) explains the thresholds to respect.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a Class 2 motorhome pay more than a car at tolls?

Yes. The multiplier applied in France is 1.7 times the car rate, which works out to around €45 more on a Paris-Marseille trip.

### What happens if my motorhome is over 3 metres tall?

It automatically tips into Class 3, with a toll rate 30 to 50% higher than a Class 2 vehicle. Always measure the height with all accessories fitted.

### Do you need a vignette to drive a motorhome in Hungary?

Yes, an electronic e-matrica vignette is mandatory for any vehicle up to 3.5 tonnes, including a Class 2 motorhome, with options from 10 days to a year.

### Does Poland's e-TOLL system apply to a Class 2 motorhome?

No. That system only applies to HGVs and coaches over 3.5 tonnes. A Class 2 motorhome pays the standard rate at existing toll booths.

### Can you pay a toll in cash in Turkey with a motorhome?

No. Turkish tolls only work through the electronic OGS or HGS systems. Without one of the two, you cannot pass through.

### Which European vignette is cheapest for a Class 2 motorhome?

Romania's Rovinieta vignette, with rates starting around €6 for 10 days, is the most affordable option among the main countries crossed on a road trip.
