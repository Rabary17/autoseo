***For a weekend in Brittany with a converted van, or a road trip to Spain, the question always comes back: how fast are you actually allowed to drive? The answer starts with one figure, the vehicle's maximum authorised weight.***

TL;DR: a motorhome under 3.5 tonnes follows ordinary car limits, 130 km/h on motorways, 80 km/h on standard roads. Above 3.5 tonnes, it switches to a regime specific to heavy passenger-transport vehicles, 110 km/h on motorways, 100 km/h on dual carriageways, 80 km/h elsewhere.

-   The **maximum authorised weight** shown in box F2 of the registration certificate sets the regime, not the vehicle's visual size.
-   With a **trailer**, the limits drop further, to 90 km/h on motorways for a combined weight of 3.5 t or less.
-   A **speed limit disc** stays recommended above 3.5 t to inform other road users, though its legal obligation is debated.

## Motorhome speed limits in France: the rules by weight

In France, a motorhome's speed limit depends on its **maximum authorised weight**, shown on the registration certificate in box F2. Two regimes coexist.

| Motorhome's max weight | Motorway | Dual carriageway | Two-lane road | Town |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ≤ 3.5 t | 130 km/h (110 in rain) | 110 km/h | 80 km/h | 50 km/h |
| > 3.5 t and ≤ 12 t | 110 km/h | 100 km/h | 80 km/h | 50 km/h |

*Source: Article R. 413-8 of the French Highway Code, decree no. 2008-754 of 30 July 2008*

A motorhome over 3.5 tonnes is **not subject to the regime for goods-carrying HGVs**, which are capped at 90 km/h on motorways. Since it carries passengers rather than freight, it falls under an intermediate, more favourable regime. That's a common mix-up, including among some rental companies.

These limits also apply to **converted vans** with VASP type approval, provided the conversion is fixed: a bed, a kitchen or storage that can't be removed. A van with a basic conversion that can be dismantled in under two hours stays classed as CTTE and follows the rules of an ordinary light vehicle, whatever its actual weight. Our guide to [fitting out a van and type approval](/pro-van-conversion-guide/) covers this distinction in detail, decisive for knowing which speed regime actually applies.

To tow a trailer, a category B licence is enough if the combined weight stays under 3.5 t. Above that, a BE licence or the B96 code becomes mandatory. Here are the limits with a trailer:

-   90 km/h on motorways for a combined weight ≤ 3.5 t;
-   80 km/h on motorways for a combined weight > 3.5 t;
-   80 km/h on standard roads, whatever the combined weight.

## Motorhome speed limits in Europe: the differences by country

Rules shift noticeably depending on which countries you cross. Here are the limits for a motorhome ≤ 3.5 t, the most common case for leisure use.

| Country | Motorway | Two-lane road | Town |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spain | 120 km/h | 90 km/h | 50 km/h |
| Germany | No limit on some sections (130 km/h recommended) | 100 km/h | 50 km/h |
| Portugal | 120 km/h | 90 km/h | 50 km/h |
| Italy | 130 km/h (110 km/h in rain) | 90 km/h | 50 km/h |
| Belgium | 120 km/h | 90 km/h | 50 km/h |

*Source: road safety authorities of the countries concerned, 2026 data*

In **Spain**, motorhomes over 3.5 t are limited to 90 km/h on motorways and 80 km/h on standard roads. In **Germany**, some motorway sections carry no limit at all, though a recommended speed of 130 km/h applies to all light vehicles. With a trailer, limits often drop by 10 to 20 km/h depending on the country. Always check local rules before a long trip; checks that specifically target motorhomes stay common during the summer season.

## Speed limit disc: what does the law actually require?

Motorhome equipment specialists strongly recommend a **kit of three discs** showing 80, 100 and 110 km/h for a motorhome over 3.5 t, to inform other road users of the maximum speed actually allowed depending on the type of road. A circle with the speed marked in black on a white background stays the expected format.

This practice draws on the obligations that apply to goods-carrying HGVs, but whether it's strictly mandatory for a motorhome remains debated among industry professionals. When in doubt, it's best to fit one anyway: it costs only a few euros and avoids any argument with the police during a check, especially abroad, where some countries enforce it more strictly, such as Spain or Italy for vehicles over 3.5 tonnes.

## Parking and camping: what's allowed (and what isn't) in France

A motorhome can park on the public road like a car, following the usual parking rules. Camping, however, stays **banned** on the public road: taking out a table, chairs, or extending an awning isn't allowed, even for a single night. This distinction matters:

-   **Allowed**: parking overnight and sleeping inside, without deploying any outdoor equipment.
-   **Not allowed**: setting up an awning, taking out furniture, or placing visible chocks or levelling blocks under the wheels.

Dedicated motorhome service areas stay the safest alternative. Some towns try to ban motorhome parking through a municipal bylaw, but these measures get overturned regularly by the courts when they aren't backed by a specific, proven risk. Our guide to [parking rules in towns](/motorhome-parking-city-rules/) covers the possible challenges in detail.

## Insurance and regulations: what to check before hitting the road

Motorhome insurance generally costs between €200 and €600 a year, depending on third-party or comprehensive cover and the vehicle's value. Before setting off, check that your policy covers **accessories** (bike racks, awning, interior equipment), often excluded from standard car policies, as well as a **trailer** if you're towing one.

For motorhomes over 3.5 t, a **category C1 licence** is generally required between 3.5 and 7.5 tonnes, except for drivers who held a category B licence before 20 January 1975, who keep that right without resitting a test. Our article on [motorhome weight and licence rules](/motorhome-weight-licence-guide/) covers the equivalences and the paperwork involved. During a check, be ready to present the registration certificate with its VASP designation for converted vans, a licence matching the vehicle's weight, and the speed limit disc if applicable.

## Tolls and other costs: budgeting correctly

Beyond speed, a heavy motorhome also shifts category at the toll booth. Motorway operators generally classify vehicles by height and weight, which can tip a converted van into class 2 or 3 rather than class 1, at a noticeably higher rate. Our guide to [Class 2 motorhome tolls](/motorhome-class-2-toll-guide/) helps you anticipate that extra cost before a long trip.

## Preparing your road trip: pre-departure checklist

Before hitting the road, check these key points:

-   Your motorhome's **maximum authorised weight**, and the trailer's if you're towing one, shown in box F2 of the registration certificate.
-   The **speed limits** in the countries you'll cross, especially for a vehicle over 3.5 t.
-   The **speed limit disc** if your vehicle is over 3.5 t, even though its obligation stays debated.
-   Your **insurance**, with cover for accessories and a trailer checked in advance.
-   Your **documents**: registration certificate, a licence matching the vehicle's weight, a current insurance certificate.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a motorhome over 3.5 tonnes drive at 90 km/h like an HGV?

No. It falls under a distinct regime reserved for passenger-transport vehicles, with 110 km/h on motorways, 100 km/h on dual carriageways and 80 km/h on standard roads, as long as its weight stays under 12 tonnes.

### Is the speed limit disc mandatory on a motorhome?

Whether it's mandatory remains debated for motorhomes, unlike for goods-carrying HGVs. Industry professionals strongly recommend it above 3.5 t, especially for trips abroad where some countries enforce it more strictly.

### What licence do you need for a motorhome over 3.5 tonnes?

A category C1 licence is generally required between 3.5 and 7.5 tonnes, except for drivers who held a category B licence before 20 January 1975, who keep that right without resitting a test.

### Can you sleep in your motorhome in a public car park?

Yes, as long as you stay inside the vehicle without deploying any outdoor equipment. Taking out a table, chairs or an awning counts as camping, which is banned outside authorised areas.

### Do speed limits change with a trailer?

Yes, they drop systematically. A motorhome-and-trailer combination under 3.5 tonnes is limited to 90 km/h on motorways, against 110 or 130 km/h without a trailer depending on the motorhome's own weight.
