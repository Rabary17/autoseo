## Why choose peer-to-peer car rental? The real cost

On a Paris-Lyon trip (465 km), a compact saloon costs an average of €80 a day with a traditional rental company. With a private owner through Getaround or Turo, expect €45 to €60. The saving generally reaches **25 to 45%**, depending on the vehicle and the season.

Another advantage is **flexibility**: platforms offer slots from one hour to several weeks, ideal for a house move or a last-minute getaway, without the fixed opening hours of an agency.

## What does the included insurance really cover?

Contrary to popular belief, [fully comprehensive insurance is mandatory and included](/peer-car-rental-insurance-gaps/) on the main peer-to-peer rental platforms. It generally covers damage to the vehicle, even when the renter is at fault, theft once a police report is filed within the contract's deadline, third-party liability up to several tens of millions of euros, and assistance plus repatriation costs after a disabling accident.

Your personal car insurance **doesn't** cover this type of rental by default. Checking with your own insurer rarely changes anything here. Only the platform's cover applies for the length of the booking.

Exclusions still apply: damage to tyres and rims outside a documented at-fault accident, theft of personal belongings left inside the car, driving under the influence, and use on a track or private circuit. In case of a claim, an **excess of €750 to €1,500** usually stays on you, depending on the contract and the protection level chosen at booking.

## What do user reviews say? 3 recurring complaints

On forums and review sites, three complaints come up often. **Variable cleanliness** tops the list: some owners don't systematically clean their car between bookings, something platforms handle case by case through customer service. **Late returns** come next: a previous renter returning the car late can push back the whole chain of following bookings, hence the value of a 30-minute buffer on pickup time. Last, a sometimes **picky vehicle check**: a scratch a few centimetres long or a tank not filled back up can trigger extra charges, which is why photos before and after each rental matter.

### How to avoid unpleasant surprises

-   Read **recent** reviews, under three months old. An owner's habits can shift from one rental to the next.
-   Check the included mileage. Some contracts cap it per day, unlike unlimited formulas elsewhere.
-   Favour cars fitted with a **connected box** where the platform offers one, for keyless entry and simpler booking tracking.

## Peer-to-peer rental vs PCP/leasing: what's the difference?

Unlike peer-to-peer carsharing, PCP (Personal Contract Purchase) and leasing run as multi-year contracts. With **PCP**, you can become the vehicle's owner at the end of the term by paying a residual value fixed at signing, for example €5,000 on a car rented at €300/month. With **leasing**, the rental stays pure, with no option to buy, and the car goes back at the end of the contract.

For a one-off need, a house move or a holiday, peer-to-peer carsharing stays far more flexible. For daily use spread over several years, PCP or leasing becomes the better option instead.

## Which mistakes should you avoid, and who is this for?

Five mistakes come up again and again. **Skipping the tyre check**: worn tyres, under 1.6 mm of tread, risk a fine and can leave the car immobilised at a roadside check. **Forgetting to refill**: some owners bill missing fuel well above the pump price. **Ignoring low-emission zones**: several cities restrict access by Crit'Air sticker, so check the car's sticker before booking if you'll drive through one. **Skipping before/after photos**: in a dispute over a scratch, photos settle it, so shoot from several angles as soon as you take the car. **Booking without comparing insurance**: cover and excesses differ by platform, a gap worth checking before a claim, not after.

Peer-to-peer rental suits one-off trips, weekends or house moves, and tight budgets, since the saving reaches 25 to 45% against a professional rental. Drivers comfortable with an app also do well here, since booking and unlocking run entirely through one. It suits you less if you need a car within the hour, since booking can take longer than at an agency, or if you're particular about cleanliness, since the risk of a poorly cleaned car never fully disappears. ## Frequently asked questions

### Does Ouicar still exist in 2026?

No. The Ouicar brand disappeared in late 2023, its activity taken over by Turo, which now operates in France with a catalogue largely inherited from the old platform.

### Does my personal car insurance cover a peer-to-peer rental?

No, not by default. These platforms impose their own fully comprehensive insurance for the length of the rental, and your personal policy generally doesn't apply in this specific case.

### What does the excess cover in case of a claim?

The excess, between €750 and €1,500 depending on the contract, is the amount that stays on you in case of damage, beyond which the platform's insurance takes over in full.

### Can you rent a car peer-to-peer for just a few hours?

Yes, most platforms offer slots from one hour, unlike traditional agencies, which usually bill a full day as a minimum.

### How do you limit the risk of a dispute when returning the car?

Take detailed photos of the car before you leave and when you return it, from several angles, and check the fuel level shown at each step to avoid any argument over its condition.
