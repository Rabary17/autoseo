***Registering an imported vehicle in France means getting a French registration certificate within one month of purchase or import. Without it, you cannot legally drive the car.***

TL;DR: budget €200 to €500 and 7 to 10 working days for a vehicle imported from an EU country with a complete file. A non-EU vehicle needing individual approval can run past €1,500 and take several extra weeks.

-   The **European Certificate of Conformity** is the document that most often blocks a file rejected by the ANTS.
-   The **French roadworthiness test** is mandatory for any imported vehicle over 4 years old, dated under 6 months at the time of the application.
-   Miss the one-month legal deadline and you risk a **fine of €135 to €750**.

Here's the exact procedure, the documents you'll need and the deadlines to meet in 2026.

## What makes an imported vehicle eligible for French registration?

**Three conditions decide eligibility**: the vehicle must meet current European standards, its foreign registration file must be complete, and it must carry no seizure or lien. An EU vehicle clears this easily through mutual recognition of conformity certificates; a non-EU vehicle usually needs individual approval instead.

American or Japanese vehicles fall into that second case, since individual approval runs more complex and costly than the standard EU route. Before anything else, check the foreign administrative status certificate for liens, and if VAT hasn't been settled yet, our guide on the [foreign vehicle tax clearance certificate](/foreign-vehicle-tax-clearance-2026/) covers that step in detail.

The **European Certificate of Conformity** stays mandatory either way: it confirms the vehicle meets EU safety and emissions standards, and a file without it gets rejected by the ANTS outright. An EU vehicle usually comes with one from the manufacturer; a non-EU vehicle needs individual approval from an authorised body instead, which costs €500 to €2,000 and can take several weeks. Our [guide to the European COC](/european-coc-vehicle-registration/) details what the document covers and where to request one.

If you already own a vehicle registered in your name in another EU country, no extra approval applies: a new French certificate only needs your existing foreign registration documents.

## Which documents and checks does your file need?

**A French roadworthiness test is mandatory for any imported vehicle over 4 years old**, dated under 6 months at the time you apply, and costing €80 to €120. It adds checks on emissions beyond the standard inspection, and a failed test means repairs before you can register the car.

The document list is strict, and an incomplete file gets rejected immediately.

| Document | Origin | Validity | Where to get it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| European Certificate of Conformity | Manufacturer or approved body | Unlimited, unless the vehicle is modified | Supplied with the vehicle or ordered separately |
| Previous foreign registration certificate | Country of origin | Must be crossed out "sold" or "exported" | Original required |
| Purchase invoice or transfer certificate | Seller or importer | Must show price and contact details | Original or certified copy |
| Proof of address under 6 months old | France | Electricity bill, phone bill, rent receipt | Utility provider or landlord |
| ID document | France | Valid | Préfecture or FranceConnect |
| French roadworthiness test under 6 months old | Approved centre in France | 6 months maximum | Roadworthiness test centre |
| Current insurance certificate | French insurer | Mandatory minimum cover | Provided online or by post |

*Source: immatriculation.ants.gouv.fr*

For a non-EU vehicle, documents not in French or English may need a sworn translation, at €50-150. All applications go through immatriculation.ants.gouv.fr: create an account, fill in Cerfa form 13750*08 online, upload the documents as PDFs under 2 MB each, then pay online.

## How much does it cost, and how long does it take?

**Expect €200 to €500 for a standard EU import**, and 7 to 10 working days once the file is complete, with the certificate posted 3 to 5 working days after approval. The region, the vehicle's taxable horsepower and any approval fees all move this figure.

| Expense | Estimated cost (2026) | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regional tax per taxable horsepower | €50-200 | Varies by region |
| Management, delivery and processing fees | €16.26 | Fixed |
| Roadworthiness test | €80-120 | Over 4 years old only |
| Sworn translation | €50-150 | Non-French/English documents |
| Individual approval (non-EU) | €500-2,000 | Non-EU vehicles only |
| Number plates | €20-50 | Two SIV-compliant plates |

*Source: immatriculation.ants.gouv.fr*

A 5-horsepower car imported from Germany and registered in Île-de-France, with a roadworthiness test and new plates, lands around €266 in total. A 7-horsepower car imported from the United States, needing individual approval and a sworn translation, climbs to roughly €1,950. Miss the one-month legal deadline after purchase or import and the fine runs €135 to €750.

## What should you do once you have your certificate, and if the ANTS rejects your file?

**Update your insurer within 15 days**, order SIV-compliant plates (€20-50 for two), and check your roadworthiness test schedule, valid 2 years under 10 years old, then annual beyond that. If you're driving on a temporary plate while the file is processed, check how long it actually holds up: a [temporary WW plate](/temporary-ww-plate-validity/) now runs on stricter, non-renewable terms.

Five mistakes cause most delays: a missing or invalid certificate of conformity, an old foreign certificate not crossed out "sold" or "exported", an error on the Cerfa form, underestimating the processing time, and forgetting to notify your insurer. Any of these, plus a vehicle that fails the roadworthiness test, is also the most common reason the ANTS rejects a file outright. A rejection email details the reason, and you then get 30 days to correct it before the application gets cancelled.

## What changes for an electric or classic vehicle?

An imported **electric or hybrid vehicle** needs a battery compliant with UN Regulation 38.3, and compatibility with French charging standards (Type 2, Combo CCS, CHAdeMO). The ecological bonus doesn't apply to a vehicle already registered abroad before import, and an EV imported from outside the EU still needs individual approval, adding €1,000-3,000 to the budget.

A **classic or vintage vehicle**, over 30 years old, follows a lighter process: no certificate of conformity required, though you'll need proof of conformity to the European standard in force at the time, and the roadworthiness test becomes optional, subject to a préfecture review. Classic vehicles can get black "collection" plates with a certificate from the FFVE or an approved club, and usually qualify for reduced insurance rates while keeping mandatory third-party cover.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long do I have to register an imported vehicle in France?

One month after purchase or import. Beyond that deadline you risk a €135 to €750 fine, and you can no longer legally drive the vehicle.

### Does the ecological bonus apply to an imported electric vehicle?

No. The bonus doesn't apply to a vehicle already registered abroad before its import into France.

### What if the European Certificate of Conformity is missing?

For an EU vehicle, request it from the manufacturer or its network. For a non-EU vehicle, individual approval from an authorised body becomes necessary, at a higher cost and over a longer timeline.

### Does an imported vehicle need a new French roadworthiness test?

Yes, if it's over 4 years old. The test must be dated under 6 months at the time you submit the registration application, carried out at an approved centre in France.
