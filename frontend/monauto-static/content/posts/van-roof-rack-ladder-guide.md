***For a tradesperson who transports a ladder every week, a van fitted with a side-mounted ladder carrier or roof rack keeps the load bay free. The right choice depends on the weight carried, how often you use it, and the legal limits.***

TL;DR: a lockable side-mounted ladder carrier suits light, occasional loads, a reinforced roof rack suits long materials and daily use. Always check the vehicle's gross vehicle weight rating before adding weight up high.

-   A **side-mounted ladder carrier** typically holds 50 to 100 kg, a **reinforced roof rack** up to 200 kg depending on the model and the vehicle's anchor points.
-   Exceeding the **gross vehicle weight (GVW)** risks a fine and a fail at the roadworthiness test.
-   An undeclared outdoor accessory can mean a **refused claim** after theft or an accident.

## Side-mounted ladder carrier or roof rack: which system for which use?

![Lockable side-mounted ladder carrier on a utility van, with an aluminium ladder loaded](/images/articles/galerie-et-porte-echelle-fourgon-1.webp)

**A side-mounted ladder carrier suits light, occasional loads, a roof rack suits heavier loads and daily use.** Two solutions dominate the market, each answering a specific need.

A **fixed side-mounted ladder carrier**, mounted on the side of the van, typically holds 50 to 100 kg and locks with a key. It suits telescopic ladders or light scaffolding for occasional use. A **roof rack** is built for heavier loads, up to 200 kg on reinforced models, and accepts roof boxes, panels or long materials like tiles or battens. Anodised aluminium models resist corrosion better than painted steel, but a poorly fitted rack can vibrate at speed and mark the bodywork.

A **rear-mounted ladder carrier**, less common, attaches to the tailgate. It suits short vans like a Renault Kangoo or Citroën Berlingo, and frees up the sides for doors or advertising wraps.

A roofer transporting tiles will go for a reinforced roof rack, accepting the loss of a few centimetres of clearance under bridges. An electrician moving conduit occasionally will settle for a lockable side-mounted ladder carrier instead, cheaper and simpler to fit.

## How much does reliable equipment cost, installation included?

**A basic side-mounted ladder carrier starts around €300-500, a reinforced roof rack often tops €800, installation included.** Prices vary significantly by material and options.

| Type of equipment | Budget (installation included) |
| --- | --- |
| Basic side-mounted ladder carrier (steel) | €300-500 |
| Lockable side-mounted ladder carrier (aluminium) | €600-900 |
| Light roof rack (up to 100 kg) | €400-700 |
| Reinforced roof rack (150-200 kg) | €800-1,200 |

These figures stay indicative: always ask the installer for a precise quote, since the final price depends on the van model, the existing fixing points and the brand chosen. Entry-level painted steel models cope poorly with moisture: rust often appears at the fixing points after two winters. For intensive use, favour anodised aluminium or stainless steel. Adjustable securing straps also cut down on rattling noise, a detail that matters on long trips.

## Does a ladder carrier or roof rack require any paperwork?

**In most cases, no, as long as the van stays CTTE and respects its gross vehicle weight.** Two points still deserve a systematic check before buying.

The **gross vehicle weight (GVW)** is the number one thing to watch. A loaded roof rack eats directly into the van's useful payload, and exceeding the GVW risks a fine and a fail at the roadworthiness test. Check the remaining payload shown on the registration certificate before adding weight up high.

**Vehicle approval** can also change if the van gets converted into a mobile workshop with a fixed bench seat and fixed equipment: it then moves into the VASP category, which changes some conditions of use. A simple ladder carrier or roof rack, with no heavy interior fit-out, doesn't trigger that category change on its own.

On **insurance**, always tell your insurer about any equipment fixed to the outside of the vehicle. An undeclared accessory can justify a refused claim after theft or an accident involving that equipment. Ask your insurer in writing whether an extension of cover applies to this type of accessory.

## DIY installation or a professional?

![Reinforced roof rack on a utility van, loaded with a ladder and roof box](/images/articles/galerie-et-porte-echelle-fourgon-2.webp)

**A side-mounted ladder carrier takes about two hours to fit with common tools, a roof rack needs more specific tools and more care.**

Essential tools include a spirit level, a torque wrench to respect the manufacturer's stated tightening torque, a metal drill bit, and silicone sealant for waterproofing. Racks generally fix to the van's original roof gutters or side rails. On a recent van, the original anchor points often take higher loads than on an older model, where you sometimes need to drill the bodywork and reinforce it with dedicated backing plates.

The most common mistakes: drilling too close to the edges, which encourages corrosion, overtightening bolts, risking bodywork deformation, or neglecting rail alignment. **The rack must stay perfectly horizontal to distribute the load correctly.**

A professional installer generally charges €150-300 to fit a roof rack, and €80-120 for a ladder carrier. The main advantage: they can issue a fitting certificate, useful at inspection or resale.

## How do you maintain the equipment so it lasts?

**Regular cleaning, checking the fixings every few thousand kilometres, and winter protection are enough to keep a rack or ladder carrier running for several years.**

After each job, clear mud and cement residue with a low-pressure water jet: abrasive products scratch anodised aluminium. Periodically retighten bolts and check the rubber mounts, since a loose bolt can let the load come free while driving. Before winter, lubricate moving parts with silicone grease, since painted steel racks cope poorly with road salt.

On road safety, a poorly secured ladder can come loose and hit another vehicle. Use ratchet straps to the EN 12195-2 standard, and check their tension before every trip. On motorways, easing off your speed slightly cuts down on vibration across the whole setup.

## When should you prefer a trailer or a tail lift instead?

**Past 200 kg of load, a dedicated trailer or a tail lift becomes more suitable than a rack or ladder carrier.**

For very heavy loads, a **material trailer** avoids overloading the van. A standard category B licence suffices up to 750 kg of gross train weight; beyond that, a category BE licence becomes mandatory. A **tail lift**, fitted at the rear of the van, lifts significantly heavier loads and suits pallet deliveries or house moves, at a budget well above a roof rack's. A watertight, lockable **roof box** stays a lighter option for protecting tools from the weather, without the constraints of a ladder carrier.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a ladder carrier void the van's manufacturer warranty?

No, as long as the installation respects the intended anchor points and doesn't alter the vehicle's structure. A badly executed drill hole, however, can justify a warranty refusal on that specific area.

### Do you need to declare a roof rack to your insurer?

Yes, always. Equipment fixed to the outside of the vehicle, left undeclared, can lead to a refused claim after theft or damage involving that accessory.

### Can you fit a roof rack on a van already converted to VASP?

Yes, this generally causes no particular issue. Just check that the added weight stays compatible with the GVW already reduced by the existing interior fit-out.

### What's the average lifespan of a well-maintained ladder carrier?

Count on several years for a properly maintained anodised aluminium model, against a shorter lifespan for a painted steel model exposed to road salt with no winter protection.

### Does the roadworthiness test check how roof equipment is fixed?

Equipment that's visibly badly fixed or dangerous can be flagged during the roadworthiness test under general vehicle safety, but there's no dedicated check specifically for a roof rack or ladder carrier.
