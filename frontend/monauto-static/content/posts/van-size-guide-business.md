***A 3m³ van typically carries 250 to 350 kg of payload, ideal for urban deliveries or a solo tradesperson's tools. A 6m³ van climbs to 1,000-1,200 kg while still clearing most 2.10 m barriers.***

Here's how to decide between the two volumes based on your routes, your budget and the tax rules that apply to utility vehicles.

TL;DR: the 3m³ suits short deliveries and solo tradespeople, the 6m³ becomes necessary for house moves and bulky equipment.

-   A **3m³** van measures roughly 1.90 to 2.10 m long for 250-350 kg of payload.
-   A **6m³** van nearly doubles the length (2.80-3.10 m) and climbs to 1,000-1,200 kg of payload.
-   The **CTTE** designation on the registration certificate opens a distinct tax regime from passenger vehicles, worth checking case by case.

## Dimensions and capacity: what each volume actually carries

On paper, 3m³ sounds modest. In practice, it's roughly the space of a single bed: about 1.90 to 2.10 m long, 1.40 m wide and 1.35 to 1.50 m high. That's enough for 8 to 10 moving boxes, a pallet of materials, or a workshop compressor.

The 6m³ nearly doubles the useful length (2.80 to 3.10 m) and gains interior height (1.85 to 2.10 m). It takes a washing machine standing upright or two bikes side by side, with no need to dismantle anything first.

| Criteria | 3m³ van | 6m³ van |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Payload | 250-350 kg | 1,000-1,200 kg |
| Useful length | 1.90-2.10 m | 2.80-3.10 m |
| Interior height | 1.35-1.50 m | 1.85-2.10 m |
| Clears a 2.10 m barrier | Yes (around 1.85 m tall) | Yes (around 2.05 m tall, model-dependent) |
| Example load | 10 boxes + 1 pallet | 1 washing machine + 2 bikes + 20 boxes |

For an electrician carrying conduit and tools, the 3m³ is usually enough. A landscaper with ride-on mowers, or a carpenter with 3-metre panels, needs the 6m³ instead, since its extra height avoids removing the rear doors on some models.

## Practical uses: when the 3m³ is enough, when the 6m³ is essential

Three scenarios where the 3m³ stays the most cost-effective choice: **urban deliveries**, a handful of parcels per round on short trips, where a compact van fits everywhere, even on narrow streets; **solo tradespeople**, a plumber or locksmith carrying tools in organised cases, where the smaller volume also helps keep fuel use down compared with a 6m³; and **service fleets**, where companies sharing several vehicles get a better usage rate from more, smaller vans than from one underused 6m³.

The 6m³ becomes essential for **house moves**, since a studio flat's furniture, sofa, bed, table and around twenty boxes, generally fits in one trip rather than two; **sites with heavy equipment**, since a cement mixer or mini excavator often exceeds a 3m³'s weight and volume limits; and **bulky furniture deliveries**, where a corner sofa or a fitted kitchen fits in standing up, with no risk of scratches from a forced load.

## Tax: what the CTTE designation changes

3m³ and 6m³ vans generally share a tax regime more favourable than passenger vehicles. The **CTTE** designation on the registration certificate places them in the utility-vehicle category, which usually sits outside the annual company-vehicle taxes (the CO2 tax and the pollutant tax, which replaced the old TVS in 2022). The exact exemption rules still depend on the vehicle's precise configuration, notably its number of seat rows, so check the exact wording on your own registration certificate before assuming an automatic exemption.

A few other points worth checking depending on your use: **VAT reclaimable** at 100% on long-term lease if the use is strictly professional; **insurance**, since a CTTE van generally costs less to insure than a VASP-approved converted vehicle like a campervan, for comparable use; and **maintenance**, where common wear parts, exhaust, pads, tyres, stay broadly in line with a passenger vehicle of equivalent size.

## Conversion and legality: watch for the VASP shift

A van converted into a mobile office or workshop must stay under certain limits to keep its CTTE designation and favourable tax regime. The points that generally tip a vehicle into the **VASP** category (specialised motor vehicle) include non-removable fixed storage, an immovable table and seating, a fixed kitchen, or a fit-out that can't be taken out quickly.

If your fit-out stays basic, a folding table, removable crates, you generally keep the CTTE designation. A van converted into a VASP-approved campervan loses that tax advantage instead, and its insurance premium often rises noticeably as a result.

This distinction matters just as much whichever volume you pick. A 3m³ van fitted out as a mobile shop, with a fixed counter and built-in storage, can tip into VASP just as easily as a 6m³ converted into a campervan. The deciding factor stays the fit-out itself, not the size of the vehicle carrying it, so check the planned layout against the VASP criteria before ordering any conversion work, whatever volume you've settled on.

## Electric or diesel: which engine for which use?

Electric vans in 2026 offer a WLTP range that varies by model and volume, generally more limited on 6m³ versions because of the added weight of the batteries. Real-world range on professional routes tends to fall short of the advertised figure, so it's worth checking actual road performance rather than the headline number before committing to a model.

Diesel stays relevant for long trips with no chance to recharge, for sites with no access to electricity, and for tighter upfront budgets, since a new diesel van generally costs less to buy than its electric equivalent, even though the gap narrows on total cost of ownership depending on annual mileage.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a 3m³ van enough for a studio move?

Not always in a single trip. A furnished studio often exceeds a 3m³'s payload and volume, forcing a second run. The 6m³ generally avoids that constraint.

### Does the CTTE designation automatically exempt a van from all tax?

No, it depends on the vehicle's precise configuration and actual use. Check the exact wording on your registration certificate rather than relying on the van's volume alone.

### Does a removable fit-out cost a van its CTTE designation?

Generally not, as long as the fit-out comes out quickly and includes no fixed bed or fixed kitchen. A fit-out that can't be removed often tips the vehicle into the VASP category.

### What's the fuel consumption difference between a 3m³ and a 6m³?

A 3m³ generally uses less fuel than a 6m³ for the same load, thanks to its lower empty weight and smaller frontal area, a point worth weighing against the extra usable volume.

### Does a 6m³ van clear every car park barrier?

Not always. Some models exceed 2.05 m in height with a raised roof. Always check the exact height of the model you're considering before driving it under a 2.10 m barrier.
