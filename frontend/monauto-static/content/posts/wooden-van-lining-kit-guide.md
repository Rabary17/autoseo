## Why choose a kit over a custom build?

**A ready-to-fit kit generally costs €350 to €1,200, against €1,500 and often over €3,000 for a custom marine plywood build.** The time saved follows the same logic: a kit installs in half a day, a custom fit-out takes several days of a professional's time.

These kits also come **modular**: removable and reinstallable in another vehicle, an advantage for businesses that regularly renew their fleet. On the tax side, a wooden lining kit stays removable equipment. It doesn't raise the vehicle's resale value and doesn't change its administrative category, unlike a heavy, permanent conversion that could tip it into the VASP category instead.

## Which van models are compatible with these kits?

**Manufacturers mainly target Europe's best-selling vans, whose standardised dimensions make pre-cut panels easier to produce.** The most common models, a Renault Kangoo Van, Peugeot Expert, Citroën Jumpy or Ford Transit Custom in their recent generations, generally get a standard kit from several specialist manufacturers, with options like sliding drawers, side shelves or reinforced flooring depending on the range.

For older models, like a first- or second-generation Citroën Berlingo, kits exist but often need adjustments: universal templates, panels you cut to size yourself, detailed instructions to avoid measurement errors. On these older generations, also check the vehicle's general condition before investing, especially past 150,000 km: a kit doesn't protect against a mechanical breakdown on an already-tired vehicle.

The van's size matters too before you invest: a kit designed for a compact van won't suit a larger model. ## How do you install a kit without drilling the bodywork?

![Detail of mounting rails and wooden panels for van lining](/images/articles/habillage-bois-utilitaire-kit-2.webp)

**Most modern kits use rail-based or structural adhesive mounting systems**, built to withstand vibration with no drilling needed. Here are the key steps for a successful install.

Start by **cleaning the interior walls thoroughly** with a degreaser; adhesives don't bond to a dirty or greasy surface. Then fit the **mounting rails**, in aluminium or galvanised steel, which anchor to the vehicle's existing fixing points. Mount the **panels**, in marine plywood or moisture-resistant MDF, which slot into the rails: high-end kits offer quick-release locking systems that need no tools. For floor panels, a polyurethane adhesive gives a durable bond while absorbing vibration. Finish with **the details**: rubber corner protectors, a silicone bead along the edges to keep water out.

To avoid drilling altogether, suction-cup or magnetic systems suit lighter fit-outs. A tradesperson carrying fragile tools will favour a kit with aluminium rails instead, able to take heavier loads per linear metre.

## What pitfalls should you avoid when buying a kit?

**The first mistake is underestimating the fit-out's weight, which directly cuts into the available payload.** A full kit generally weighs 40 to 80 kg depending on the vehicle's size, which can take up a significant share of payload on a compact van like a Kangoo. Past the authorised GVW, the vehicle risks a fine at a roadside check, and the insurer can also challenge cover after an accident. **Before buying, always check the remaining payload, GVW minus kerb weight, shown on the registration certificate, box F2.**

Another common pitfall: overlooking approval status. A wooden lining kit on its own doesn't change the vehicle's administrative category, which stays CTTE in principle. It's the heavier transformations, like adding a fixed bench seat or a built-in kitchen, that can tip the vehicle into the VASP category instead, with stricter roadworthiness checks and different traffic rules. To avoid that risk, favour removable kits certified by their manufacturer over a structural transformation. Our article on [approving a van towing hitch](/van-towing-hitch-regulations-2026/) details a related case of equipment modification subject to checks.

A last pitfall worth flagging: buying on dimensions alone. A kit sized for a short-wheelbase van won't fit a long-wheelbase version of the same model, even though the badge on the back looks identical. Always confirm the exact wheelbase and roof height with the seller before ordering, and ask whether the kit ships with adjustable rails for small tolerances between model years. A kit that arrives a few centimetres short is harder to return once cut panels have been trimmed to fit, so measure the load area yourself before ordering rather than trusting the model name alone.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a wooden lining kit void the van's manufacturer warranty?

No, in most cases, as long as the fitting respects the existing anchor points with no drilling or structural change. Check the exact terms with the manufacturer before fitting one.

### Do you need to declare a wooden lining kit to your insurer?

It's generally not mandatory for a standard removable kit, but flag it as a precaution if its weight or value is significant, to avoid any dispute after a claim.

### Does a wooden lining kit suit a vehicle already in the VASP category?

Yes, this generally causes no particular issue, as long as you check the added weight stays compatible with the GVW already reduced by the existing interior fit-out.

### Can you install a kit yourself with no DIY experience?

Most kits are designed for installation by anyone with basic tools, following the instructions. A kit that needs drilling or specific adapters takes more experience, though.

### How long does a well-maintained wooden lining kit last?

A kit in marine plywood or aluminium, properly maintained and kept away from excess moisture, generally lasts several years with no warping, even under regular road vibration.
