2026, and electric vans finally offer ranges that make sense—provided you pick the right model. Between a Renault Kangoo, which will do the job for urban deliveries, and a Mercedes eSprinter, built for long-haul routes, the choice depends on your actual needs, not the manufacturer’s figures. Here’s how to avoid the pitfalls of discontinued incentives and set up charging without blowing your budget.

## 1\. Real-World Range: What WLTP Figures Don’t Tell You

![Electric Renault Kangoo E-Tech van making a delivery on a country road](/images/articles/utilitaires-electriques-1.webp)

The official ranges (250 to 460 km WLTP) paint a rosier picture than reality. For a daily 150 km run in the Paris region, a Renault Kangoo E-Tech (285 km WLTP) will do the job, but with little margin for error in winter or when fully loaded. Conversely, a Mercedes eSprinter (440 km WLTP) suits long-haul routes, but its price and size make it viable only for larger fleets.

Two often-overlooked factors:

-   **Real-world consumption**: A van loaded to 80% of its capacity will see its range drop by 20–30% compared to the manufacturer’s figures. Always test the model with your typical load before buying.
-   **Fast charging**: Not all models support 100 kW charging. A Peugeot e-Expert, for example, maxes out at 50 kW—a detail to check if your routes require mid-day top-ups.

## 2\. Purchase Incentives: What Changed in 2026 (and What’s Left)

The **ecological bonus for businesses was scrapped entirely** on 1 January 2025. No more direct purchase incentives for legal entities—a move that caught many SMEs off guard. In Île-de-France, the region still offers a €3,000 grant for fleets of fewer than 10 vehicles, provided you buy through an approved dealer. Elsewhere, here’s what remains:

-   **The scrappage scheme**: If you scrap an old diesel van (Crit’Air 3 or worse), you can still claim up to €5,000, though micro-businesses must meet income criteria.
-   **Tax benefits**: VAT remains 100% reclaimable for vans (vs. 50% for passenger vehicles), and the Company Vehicle Tax (TVS) doesn’t apply to CTTE (light commercial vehicle) models.

## 3\. Charging a Fleet: Solutions That Don’t Require Reinventing the Wheel

![Charging a fleet of electric vans in a company car park](/images/articles/utilitaires-electriques-2.webp)

Installing chargers for a fleet demands a proper strategy. Three practical scenarios:

-   **Overnight charging on private premises**: The simplest solution. Expect to pay €1,500–€3,000 per charger (7–22 kW), with ADEME (French Environment and Energy Management Agency) grants covering up to 50% of costs. Ideal for fleets that operate by day and charge at night.
-   **Daytime charging at client sites**: If your teams work at client locations with chargers, a Chargemap Pro card avoids heavy infrastructure investment. Check network coverage in your operating areas.
-   **Motorway fast charging**: Essential for long routes. Models compatible with 100 kW (like the Ford E-Transit) can recover 80% charge in 30 minutes, but watch the costs (€0.50–€0.70/kWh for rapid charging).

One often-missed point: power management. A fleet of 10 vans charging simultaneously at 22 kW requires a 220 kW subscribed capacity—meaning prohibitive EDF subscription costs. The fix? Smart chargers that smooth out demand or buffer batteries to reduce peak load.

## 5\. Mistakes to Avoid When Switching to Electric Vans

![Business owner reviewing costs of an electric van fleet](/images/articles/utilitaires-electriques-3.webp)

-   **Underestimating winter charging**: At -5°C, range can drop by 30%. Build in a safety margin, especially for long routes.
-   **Skipping driver training**: Eco-driving (anticipation, regenerative braking) can boost range by 10–15%. A one-day course often pays for itself.
-   **Forgetting charger installation grants**: ADEME offers subsidies for businesses, but applications must be submitted before work begins. Your local Chamber of Commerce [\[link to hub régions\]](https://example.com) lists up-to-date local schemes.
-   **Choosing a model that doesn’t fit your routes**: A van with 250 km range works for urban trips but won’t suit 300 km daily runs. Analyse your actual routes before deciding.

## 6\. Leasing vs. Buying: Which Financing Option Works Best for Your Fleet?

Long-term leasing (LLD) is increasingly popular with businesses—and for good reason:

-   **Tax benefits**: Lease payments are 100% deductible from taxable profits, and VAT is fully reclaimable for vans (exclusive business use).
-   **No depreciation risk**: The vehicle is returned at the end of the contract, with no resale hassle.
-   **Maintenance included**: Most LLD contracts cover servicing and tyres, simplifying fleet management.

Buying only makes sense if you cover over 30,000 km/year and keep vehicles for at least five years. Otherwise, leasing is simpler and often cheaper in the long run.

A concrete example: For a Renault Kangoo E-Tech, leasing costs around €500/month over 48 months (with maintenance). Buying would total roughly €40,000 (excluding depreciation)—a €4,000 premium for leasing, but with simpler management and no risk.

## 7\. Where to Buy Your Electric Van? The Best Options in 2026

Several routes are open to you:

-   **Authorised dealers**: Renault, Peugeot, Ford, and Mercedes offer full electric van ranges, with financing tailored to businesses. Perk: manufacturer warranties (typically 8 years on the battery).
-   **Long-term leasing companies**: Arval, LeasePlan, or ALD Automotive provide LLD contracts with recent, well-maintained vehicles. Ideal for businesses that don’t want to handle resale.
-   **Professional used-van platforms**: Spoticar (Renault’s network) or the [Domaine auctions](https://enchères-domaine.gouv.fr) offer used electric vans with warranties. A good bet for tight budgets—just check the battery’s condition (remaining capacity, charging history).
-   **Electric-vehicle specialists**: Companies like Beev or Electra offer turnkey solutions (purchase + charger installation). Useful for fleets that want to outsource everything.

One tip: Always request a test drive with your typical load. An electric van handles differently from a diesel—especially in terms of acceleration and regenerative braking.

## 8\. Licences, Registration, and Taxes: What Changes with an Electric Van

Switching to electric doesn’t change the paperwork, but a few key points:

-   **Licence**: A standard car licence (B) is enough for an electric van, provided its gross vehicle weight (PTAC) doesn’t exceed 3.5 tonnes. Above that, a Category C licence is required.
-   **Registration**: The CTTE (light commercial vehicle) designation in section J.1 of the registration certificate is mandatory to qualify for tax benefits (TVS exemption, VAT reclaim).
-   **Taxes**: Electric vans are exempt from the Company Vehicle Tax (TVS) and the ecological penalty. However, the regional registration tax still applies (around €50 on average).
-   **Low-emission zones**: Electric vans qualify for a green Crit’Air sticker, granting unrestricted access to all Low-Emission Zones (ZFE).
