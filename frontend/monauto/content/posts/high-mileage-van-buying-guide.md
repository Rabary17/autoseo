Contrary to popular belief, mileage alone doesn't determine how much life a vehicle has left. Here's how to secure your purchase, verify its history, and make the most of the tax benefits.

## High mileage: a risk or an opportunity?

High mileage, over 150,000 km, doesn't necessarily mean a van is near the end of its life. Modern diesel engines, like Ford's 2.0 TDCi EcoBlue or Peugeot's 1.6 BlueHDi, are built to reach 250,000 to 300,000 km. The real risk lies in a patchy service history or a tampered odometer, a fraud that affects a non-negligible share of used vehicles sold in France according to the DGCCRF (the French consumer-fraud authority).

For a professional buyer, a high-mileage van can mean real savings: the purchase price often sits 30 to 50% below a newer model. The tax side stays attractive too, with an exemption from the annual company-vehicle taxes for CTTE-classified vehicles and recoverable VAT on long-term leases.

### How to check if the mileage is genuine

Four checks limit the risk of buying a tampered odometer: the **full service book**, whose recorded mileages should match the dates of the work carried out; the **HistoVec history report**, free and provided by the Ministry of the Interior, which cross-references MOT data; the **wear on parts** (steering wheel, pedals, gearstick) compared with the stated mileage; and an **electronic diagnostic** of the engine control unit, which logs the real mileage on most recent models.

If you discover the mileage was tampered with after buying, the law protects you. You can take legal action for a hidden defect or fraud, and courts regularly order sellers to refund the buyer, even when they claim to have been victims themselves of a previous owner's tampering.

Not all vans are equal when it comes to high mileage. Here are the points to watch by model, based on professional feedback and available reliability data.

Conversely, some models deserve extra caution even at moderate mileage, when they carry known issues with the timing chain or excessive oil consumption. Always ask for the full service book before committing.

### Where to buy a reliable used van

-   **Government auctions** (encheres-domaine.gouv.fr): vehicles from public-sector fleets, often well maintained, at prices below market average.
-   **Specialist dealerships** (multi-brand networks such as Distinxion or Jean Lain Occasions): extended warranties and a full history supplied.
-   **Online platforms** (Leboncoin, La Centrale): insist on a HistoVec report and an MOT less than 6 months old, and be wary of listings that look too good to be true.

## Tax benefits: how to get the most from your purchase

**The TVS company-vehicle tax no longer exists since 1 January 2022.** It was replaced by two annual taxes on passenger cars: one on CO₂ emissions and one on air-pollutant emissions, which replaced the old age-based tax in 2024. Both only apply to vehicles classified as passenger cars (VP), not light commercial vehicles.

Light commercial vehicles marked **CTTE** on the registration certificate stay exempt from both taxes, regardless of their engine. This exemption applies to both purchase and long-term leasing, where lease payments are also fully deductible from taxable profit. VAT stays 100% recoverable if the vehicle is used exclusively for business.

For passenger vehicles held by a business, the two taxes apply on a scale that changes every year. The ecological penalty on a new vehicle purchase, meanwhile, starts in 2026 from 108 g/km of CO₂ and can reach €80,000 above 191 g/km, a noticeably lower entry threshold than in previous years.

### Ecological bonus: scrapped for businesses

Since **2 December 2024**, the ecological bonus for buying an electric vehicle has been scrapped for legal entities, whether a passenger car or a new light commercial vehicle. Only private individuals can still claim it, subject to income conditions. Some local grants for businesses still exist, notably in the Paris region; check with your regional authority before buying.

### Depreciation and tax deduction

A used van is generally depreciated over 4 to 5 years depending on its age at purchase. Businesses can deduct the purchase price, excluding recoverable VAT, from their taxable profit, following declining-balance or straight-line depreciation rules. For a vehicle over 5 years old, straight-line depreciation over 5 years is the most common practice.

## Licence, registration and paperwork

A **category B licence is enough** to drive a van with a GVW of no more than 3.5 tonnes, carrying a maximum of 9 seats including the driver. This covers most compact vans and light commercials. Beyond 3.5 tonnes, a category C licence becomes necessary.

After buying, you have **one month** to change the registration certificate into your name, or risk a fine of €135 to €750. Prepare Cerfa form 13750*08, proof of address less than 6 months old, the previous registration certificate crossed out and signed, and an MOT less than 6 months old if the vehicle is over 4 years old. Apply online via immatriculation.ants.gouv.fr, with processing in 7 to 10 days and a total cost generally between €200 and €500 depending on the region.

## Maintenance and warning lights to watch

![Van dashboard displaying the orange maintenance warning light (spanner symbol)](/images/articles/utilitaire-occasion-kilometrage-2.webp)

A high-mileage van needs rigorous upkeep to avoid costly breakdowns: brake pads and discs every 40,000 to 50,000 km for intensive urban use, a particulate filter to watch every 100,000 to 150,000 km on repeated short trips, a timing belt to change every 100,000 to 160,000 km depending on the model, and fluids (engine oil every 15,000 to 20,000 km, coolant and brake fluid every two years).

| Warning light | Colour | Meaning | Recommended action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spanner | Orange | Maintenance due | Schedule a service as soon as possible |
| Engine | Orange | Engine or emissions fault | Get the vehicle diagnosed quickly |
| Oil | Red | Low oil level or insufficient pressure | Stop the vehicle and get it checked immediately |
| Brake | Red | Braking system issue | Have the system inspected without delay |

## Buying or long-term leasing: which option should you choose?

Long-term leasing (LLD) avoids maintenance hassles and the risk of depreciation, with fixed payments that often include servicing and breakdown cover. It gives access to newer vehicles, changed every 3 to 5 years, but imposes an annual mileage cap and often costs more than buying over the full period of ownership.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a van with 200,000 km on the clock a good deal?

Yes, if the service book is complete and the HistoVec report is consistent. A well-maintained Ford Transit Custom or Renault Kangoo can still cover another 150,000 km without a major breakdown.

### How can you tell if a van's odometer has been tampered with?

Compare the displayed mileage against past MOT results via HistoVec, check wear on the steering wheel and pedals, and ask for an electronic diagnostic of the engine control unit.

### Does a CTTE van still pay the old company-vehicle tax?

That tax no longer exists since 2022, replaced by two annual taxes on passenger cars. CTTE-classified vans stay exempt from both, regardless of their engine.

### Can businesses still get the ecological bonus on an electric van?

No, this grant was scrapped for legal entities from 2 December 2024. Only private individuals can still claim it, subject to income conditions.

### How long do you have to change the registration certificate after buying?

One month from the purchase date. Beyond that, the fine ranges from €135 to €750 depending on the case.

### Buying or leasing: which is better for a high-mileage van?

Buying suits drivers who cover a lot of ground and keep their vehicle for several years. Leasing suits those who prefer a newer vehicle with no resale hassle, within an annual mileage cap.
