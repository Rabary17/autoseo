***A motorhome whose maximum authorised weight stays under 3.5 tonnes drives on a standard Category B licence. Beyond that, Category C stays mandatory in 2026, except for one historical exception.***

A motorhome's weight directly decides which licence you need to drive it. An old exception, the B79 endorsement, and a European reform now being transposed change the picture for some drivers. Here are the exact rules to check before buying a vehicle or booking a road trip.

TL;DR: a Category B licence covers up to 3.5 tonnes of maximum authorised weight, Category C becomes mandatory beyond that, except for licences issued before 1975 with the B79 endorsement.

-   The Category B limit stays fixed at **3,500 kg** of maximum authorised weight in 2026.
-   The **B79** endorsement, for licences issued before 20 January 1975, allows a higher weight without Category C.
-   An EU directive, due to be transposed before **November 2027**, could raise this threshold to 4.25 tonnes for some motorhomes.

## What's the 3.5-tonne rule for a motorhome?

**A Category B licence covers driving a motorhome whose maximum authorised weight doesn't exceed 3,500 kg, whatever the driver's age.**

This limit applies to a motorhome the same way it applies to an ordinary car. A low-profile or coachbuilt motorhome under 3.5 tonnes falls into this category. A converted van, generally lighter, usually stays comfortably under this threshold.

A vehicle that exceeds this limit requires a Category C licence, a rule confirmed by the French Federation of Camping and Caravanning (FFCC). The only exception: a Category B licence issued before 20 January 1975, carrying the **B79** endorsement. That allows a maximum authorised weight above 3.5 tonnes without a Category C licence.

This weight limit matters well beyond the test centre. It also shapes how much the vehicle costs to run day to day: a motorhome's category at the toll booth, its insurance premium and even its depreciation curve all shift once it crosses that 3.5-tonne line, so it's worth checking the figure in box F2 of the registration certificate before you fall in love with a particular layout.

## What does the B79 endorsement actually change?

If your Category B licence dates from before 20 January 1975, check the back of it for the B79 endorsement. It allows you to drive a motorhome whose maximum authorised weight exceeds 3.5 tonnes without taking a Category C licence, a real advantage for owners of older or heavily fitted-out vehicles.

Three conditions apply together: the licence must date from before 1975, the B79 endorsement must appear explicitly on it, and no further steps are needed since the endorsement stays valid for life. Always check for this endorsement before signing for a heavy vehicle.

## What if your motorhome exceeds 3.5 tonnes?

Two options exist for a driver whose vehicle exceeds the limit without a B79 endorsement. **Taking the Category C licence** stays the most direct route, with a theory test and a practical exam to pass. **Lightening the vehicle**, by cutting back on fittings or choosing lighter materials, sometimes brings it back under the 3.5-tonne mark.

The B96 endorsement also lets you tow a trailer over 750 kg, provided the combined weight of vehicle and trailer doesn't exceed 4,250 kg, a useful option for bringing along a car or a motorbike. This endorsement comes from a short 7-hour training course with no exam, unlike the BE licence, for a trailer over 3,500 kg, which requires a full practical test.

## What rules apply to campervans and converted vans?

A campervan or converted van doesn't escape weight rules, but benefits from an added allowance tied to **VASP type approval**. This becomes mandatory once the conversion meets five combined criteria: fixed storage, a table with seating, seats that convert into a bed or a fixed bed, a fixed kitchen (gas, electric or diesel), and fittings that can't be removed in under two hours.

A basic conversion that can be dismantled quickly doesn't need this approval, a solution that suits travellers who prioritise flexibility. Without VASP approval where it's actually required, an insurer can refuse to cover a claim, and a roadside check can penalise an undeclared conversion with a fine. Always check the vehicle's compliance before setting off.

## What changes with insurance and parking?

A motorhome needs tailored insurance, generally between **€200 and €600 a year** depending on the cover chosen, third-party or fully comprehensive, and the vehicle's value.

On parking, a motorhome can park on the public road like a car, provided the parking isn't dangerous or obstructive. Bylaws that specifically ban motorhomes stay unlawful, though a limited and justified restriction can still hold up, as our guide to [parking rules in towns](/motorhome-parking-city-rules/) explains in detail. "Camping," however, using levelling blocks, extending an awning, or taking out a table and chairs on the public road, stays banned everywhere. To avoid trouble, favour dedicated areas or campsites. ## Is the 4.25-tonne Category B licence coming soon for motorhomes?

**Yes, an EU directive transposed into French law before November 2027 should raise the Category B threshold to 4.25 tonnes for some motorhomes.**

The "driving licence" directive no. 2025/2205 provides that Category B holders could drive vehicles with a maximum authorised weight between 3.5 and 4.25 tonnes, and up to 5 tonnes with a trailer under certain conditions. A new code, 96.02, and roughly 7 hours of training are mentioned to frame this widened access. Talks between France's road safety delegation and the FFCC were still ongoing as of September 2026 over the precise transposition details.

Until the French text is published in the Official Journal, the 3.5-tonne rule stays fully in force. The B79 exception keeps applying to older licences, and any motorhome over 3.5 tonnes without that endorsement still requires a Category C1 licence today. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can you drive a 3.5-tonne motorhome with a standard Category B licence?

Yes, with no restriction linked to the driver's age or when they passed their test, as long as the vehicle's maximum authorised weight doesn't exceed 3,500 kg.

### What is the B79 endorsement on a driving licence?

It applies to Category B licences issued before 20 January 1975 and allows driving a vehicle whose maximum authorised weight exceeds 3.5 tonnes, without needing a Category C licence.

### Is the 4.25-tonne Category B licence already in force in France?

No, not yet in 2026. The EU directive must be transposed into French law before November 2027, so the 3.5-tonne rule stays in force in the meantime.

### Can you tow a trailer with just a Category B licence?

Yes, up to 750 kg with no extra training. Beyond that, the B96 endorsement, gained through 7 hours of training with no exam, allows a combination up to 4,250 kg.

### Does a converted van have to carry VASP type approval?

Only if the conversion meets all five combined criteria: fixed storage, a table and seating, a fixed or convertible bed, a fixed kitchen, and fittings that can't be removed in under two hours.

### What's the risk of driving a motorhome too heavy for your Category B licence?

An offence for driving without the licence required for the vehicle's weight, with criminal consequences and a likely refusal from your insurer if something goes wrong, since the policy generally doesn't cover a driver not qualified for that weight.
