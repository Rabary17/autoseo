***Standard car insurance never covers a peer-to-peer rental, not even with a fully comprehensive policy. Getaround and Citiz each bundle their own cover, but every one carries its own excess and exclusions worth knowing before you book.***

Thinking of renting a car through Getaround or booking a Citiz vehicle on your usual insurance? Bad news: your insurer treats this as a different use entirely. The platforms step in with their own cover, but you still need to know what it actually pays for, and what it leaves on your shoulders.

TL;DR:

-   An undeclared use, like a **driver who isn't listed on the booking**, often cancels the claim outright.
-   Getaround's excess can climb to **€1,000** after an at-fault accident.
-   Citiz works on a different model, with **no excess** for most normal use.
-   Rental income from your own car stays **taxable**, whatever platform you use.

## Why doesn't standard car insurance cover peer-to-peer rental?

A personal car policy is priced for private use, not for a car handed over to a stranger for money. The moment a vehicle changes hands through a platform, the insurer reclassifies it as a **change of use**, even for a single-day booking.

In practice, a renter who causes an accident in your own car gets no help from your personal policy. The insurer invokes the usage clause and refuses to pay, leaving you to cover repairs that can run into thousands of euros.

That's exactly why Getaround and Citiz both build a **fully comprehensive insurance** into the rental itself. It switches on automatically for the length of the booking, with nothing to tell your own insurer. For how the two platforms compare overall, see our [Getaround vs Turo comparison](/getaround-vs-ouicar-comparison/).

The same logic runs both ways. If you own the car and rent it out, your personal policy still doesn't cover a stranger behind the wheel. The platform's insurance becomes the only valid protection, for the renter and for you.

## Which insurance covers a Getaround rental?

![Renter checking a car's condition before a peer-to-peer rental](/images/articles/assurance-location-entre-particuliers-1.webp)

Getaround bundles fully comprehensive cover, arranged with a partner insurer, directly into the rental price. Three guarantees stand out: **damage to the rented car** even when the renter is at fault, **third-party liability** toward others, and theft under conditions, with a police report required within 48 hours.

That cover still has real limits. The **excess** can reach €1,000 depending on the model booked. A driver who isn't listed on the contract, like a friend who borrows the car, is never covered. And any claim must be reported within **5 working days**, after which cover can be flatly refused.

Before booking, always read the terms specific to the car you've chosen. They vary by model and by rental length. Our [peer-to-peer car rental guide](/peer-to-peer-car-rental-guide/) walks through other common pitfalls on these platforms.

## Which insurance covers a Citiz carsharing booking?

Citiz runs on fixed stations shared by several members, and it works differently. The included cover has **no excess** for damage to the vehicle, as long as you stick to the subscription's terms of use.

Liability toward others even extends to **use outside the booking window**, say a personal trip tacked on after the reservation ends. Citiz still requires you to hold your own car insurance to sign up, even though that policy never covers the rental itself.

On pricing, the subscription at €16/month plus from €3/hour, or the no-commitment plan at €5.50/hour, both include this cover at no extra charge. Our [Citiz 2026 rates guide](/citiz-car-sharing-rates-2026/) breaks down the real cost depending on how often you drive.

This fixed-station model also changes how minor claims get handled. A car shared by several members picks up small scratches now and then, logged and photographed at each booking. Citiz absorbs that kind of everyday wear, unlike a standard policy that would hold the driver accountable.

## What pitfalls help you avoid a claim refusal?

Most disputes come from a detail overlooked before or during the rental, not from an actual coverage gap. Four habits cut the risk sharply.

-   **Photograph the car** before you drive off, scratches and chips included. Without proof, you can be held responsible for damage that was already there.
-   **Follow the terms of use**: a trip abroad, an unpaved road, a driver under 21, any of these can void cover if they aren't allowed under the booking.
-   **Never hand the car to someone else**. Only the renter named on the booking stays covered.
-   **Report any claim straight away**, ideally within the hour. Once the contractual deadline passes, cover becomes hard to argue for.

A common case shows the risk clearly: a deposit withheld after an accident reported too late, despite photos taken before and after the rental. The platform leans on the missed deadline, not on a lack of proof.

This kind of dispute stays rare when the procedure is followed to the letter. Most refusals come from a simple oversight, not a platform unwilling to pay. A complete file, submitted on time, almost always gets a fair payout.

## What if the insurer refuses to cover a claim?

**Start by re-reading the terms** to check the claim genuinely falls within what's covered. A lot of refusals trace back to a misread clause rather than a real dispute.

If the platform still refuses after you've raised it with customer service, escalate to the **insurance ombudsman**, a free and independent service reachable through [mediation-assurance.org](https://mediation-assurance.org). Legal action remains the last resort, slow and costly, best kept for disputes of real financial weight.

Keep every piece of evidence: timestamped photos, written exchanges, the accident report. Without them, challenging a refusal against a platform becomes close to impossible.

## Renting out your own car: which insurance applies?

If you list your own car on a platform, your personal policy excludes that use too. You must take out the **insurance the platform offers**, built into the rental agreement, and it isn't optional.

Check your car loan terms as well if the vehicle isn't fully paid off. Some lenders ban renting out a financed vehicle. Income from peer-to-peer rental also stays taxable, declared under non-commercial profits.

A common example makes the stakes clear. An owner renting their car out ten days a month can easily bring in several hundred euros a year, but pays tax on that income in return, on top of faster wear on the vehicle, both worth planning for before you start.

A **top-up insurance** can be worth it if you rent out regularly. It covers lost rental income while the car is off the road after an accident, or damage from a renter the platform's base cover doesn't reach. Check with your own insurer to compare what's on offer.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does my personal car insurance cover a Getaround rental?

No. A standard car policy excludes renting the car to someone else. Only the cover built in by Getaround or Citiz actually applies to this use, for the renter and for the owner alike.

### What's the excess if I have an accident in a Getaround car?

It can reach €1,000 depending on the model booked. Always check this figure in the terms specific to the car before you confirm the booking.

### Can a friend drive a car I rented peer-to-peer?

No, unless they're listed as a second driver on the booking. A driver who isn't declared is never covered by the platform's insurance.

### Does Citiz charge an excess like Getaround?

Generally not, for use that follows the subscription's terms. That's one of the main differences from a Getaround peer-to-peer rental.

### Do I have to declare income from renting out my car?

Yes. That income is taxable under non-commercial profits, whatever amount you earn through the platform over the year.
