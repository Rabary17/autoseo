***A towing hitch fitted to a van must carry a riveted approval plate, respect the vehicle's gross vehicle weight (GVW), and appear on the registration certificate as soon as it changes that GVW.***

A tradesperson towing a tool trailer with a Ford Transit Custom, a courier adding a trailer on busy delivery days: in both cases, the hitch must meet precise strength and certification standards. Without that, the roadworthiness test refuses the vehicle, and insurance can refuse to pay out after an accident.

TL;DR: an unapproved hitch risks a €135 fine and a fail at the roadworthiness test.

-   The **UNECE R55** standard governs any hitch fitted to a vehicle registered after 1998.
-   Installation costs **€500 to €1,200** for a light commercial van.
-   A change to the GVW requires a **registration certificate update**, for €4.

## Why is hitch approval mandatory, and which standards apply?

An uncertified hitch can detach while driving and cause an accident or damage the vehicle's structure. European Directive **94/20/EC** and UNECE Regulation R55 require tension, torsion and vertical load tests before any hitch goes on the market.

A hitch for a van like a Transit Custom must withstand a towing force of 1,500 kg without deforming. During a roadside check, police inspect the **riveted plate** on the hitch, which shows the approval number and the maximum authorised GVW. Its absence means immediate immobilisation of the vehicle.

Three texts govern a hitch fitted in France. **UNECE R55** sets the strength requirements for any vehicle registered after 1998. The **NF R18-510** standard adds further rules for detachable ball hitches. **Directive 2007/46/EC** organises general vehicle and equipment approval.

A hitch must also respect the vehicle's GVW. A van capped at 2.8 tonnes can't tow a 3.5-tonne trailer, even with a hitch approved for that load. Exceeding the GVW risks a €135 fine and a 3-point licence penalty. ## How do you get a hitch approved for a van?

![Mechanic installing a towing hitch on a white van in a workshop](/images/articles/attelage-utilitaire-homologation-1.webp)

Approval follows four steps, in this order.

1.  **Choose a certified hitch**, already approved for your exact vehicle model.
2.  **Have it fitted by a professional**, who checks compatibility and attaches the approval plate.
3.  **Pass the roadworthiness test**, which validates the plate and the fitting.
4.  **Update the registration certificate**, if the hitch changes the vehicle's GVW.

Installation costs **€500 to €1,200** for a van like a Peugeot Expert, depending on the hitch model chosen. Updating the registration certificate, when the GVW changes, costs €4 and is handled online through the ANTS website.

## What are the risks of an unapproved hitch?

Driving with a non-compliant hitch exposes you to several penalties that can stack up.

-   **€135 fine** for lack of approval or exceeding the GVW.
-   **Vehicle immobilisation** if an immediate safety risk is found on the road.
-   **Roadworthiness test failure**, which blocks the vehicle's validation.
-   **Partial insurance voidance** for an accident directly linked to the hitch.

These penalties add to the cost of bringing the vehicle back into compliance and to the downtime involved, a cost often underestimated for a fleet of several vans.

## Which vans can be fitted, and what's the tax impact for businesses?

Any light commercial vehicle in the **CTTE** category can take a towing hitch, provided its GVW stays respected. Towing capacity varies a lot between models, worth checking before buying any trailer.

| Model | GVW (kg) | Max towing capacity (kg) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Renault Kangoo Van (2021+) | 2,000 | 1,300 |
| Peugeot Expert (2016+) | 2,800 | 2,000 |
| Citroën Jumpy (2016+) | 2,800 | 2,000 |
| Ford Transit Custom (2018+) | 3,200 | 2,500 |

A van that tows heavy loads regularly also benefits from proven long-term engine reliability, a point covered in our comparison of [3m³ against 6m³ vans](/van-size-guide-business/).

A van fitted with a towing hitch keeps its exemption from the annual company-vehicle taxes (the CO2 tax and the pollutant tax, which replaced the old TVS in 2022), as long as the CTTE designation stays on the registration certificate. If the hitch pushes the vehicle's GVW past 3.5 tonnes, the tax can apply instead, since the vehicle then falls outside the exemption criteria.

For a business on a long-term lease, hitch rental payments are deductible from taxable income, and VAT is fully recoverable if the van serves exclusively professional use.

## Where do you buy an approved hitch, fixed or detachable?

A dealership offers approved hitches with guaranteed fitting and an automatic registration certificate update. Equipment specialists like Brink or Westfalia make kits tailored to each model, detachable ball hitches included.

Before any online purchase, always check for **UNECE R55** on the product page, along with exact compatibility with your model. A hitch designed for a Renault Kangoo won't necessarily fit a Peugeot Expert, even when the dimensions look similar.

Public auctions sometimes offer second-hand hitches from decommissioned public fleets, at reduced prices. Always check the approval plate is legible before bidding; a missing or worn plate makes the equipment legally unusable.

Then comes the choice between the two main types. A **fixed hitch** suits a business towing a trailer daily, for site materials, say. It holds up better under repeated loads and needs less upkeep than a locking system. A **detachable ball hitch** suits a fleet alternating between city deliveries and towing trips: the ball comes off in seconds, which avoids getting in the way during parking manoeuvres or at an automatic car wash. It typically costs €100-200 more than an equivalent fixed model, a premium usually worth it for occasional use. The choice also depends on how often the trailer gets used: a business towing several times a week gains from a sturdier fixed system, while a tradesperson towing occasionally favours the detachable model's discretion.

## Mistakes to avoid

Four mistakes come up most often among businesses equipping their fleet.

-   **Mixing up passenger-vehicle and van hitches**: a hitch designed for a passenger vehicle doesn't suit a van, since the strength standards differ.
-   **Forgetting the registration certificate**: if the hitch changes the GVW, the update becomes mandatory, or the vehicle stays non-compliant.
-   **Leaving the installation to an amateur**: a poorly executed fitting can let the hitch detach while driving.
-   **Skipping the roadworthiness test**: this step validates the whole setup after fitting.

For a professional fleet, these oversights cost time and avoidable compliance fees. Following each step carefully limits this kind of incident.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does an unapproved hitch block the roadworthiness test?

Yes. The inspector checks that the approval plate is present and legible. Without it, the vehicle gets a mandatory re-inspection before it can be presented again.

### Do you need to declare a hitch to your insurer?

Yes, especially if it changes the vehicle's GVW. An undeclared hitch can justify a refused claim for an accident directly linked to the towed trailer.

### Does a detachable ball hitch follow a different approval process?

No, it follows the same UNECE R55 and NF R18-510 standards as a fixed hitch. Only the removal mechanism differs, with no impact on technical certification.

### How much does updating the registration certificate cost after fitting a hitch?

€4 online through the ANTS website, only if the hitch changes the vehicle's GVW. With no change to the GVW, no step is needed.

### Can you fit a second-hand hitch to a van?

Yes, provided you check the approval plate and its match with the vehicle's exact GVW. A second-hand hitch with no legible plate has no legal value.
